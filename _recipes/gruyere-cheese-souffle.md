---
title: Gruyère Cheese Soufflé
date: 2026-07-30 09:00:04 -0400
description: A béchamel base lightened with whipped egg whites, baked until it climbs well above the rim — and eaten before it has the chance to fall.
image: /assets/images/Gruyere-Cheese-Souffle.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 20 min
cook_time: 25 min
tags: [cheese, eggs, baking, vegetarian, main]
ingredients:
  - 3 tbsp | unsalted butter, plus more for ramekins
  - 2 tbsp | grated Parmesan, for ramekins
  - 3 tbsp | all-purpose flour
  - 1 cup | whole milk, warmed
  - 4 | large eggs, separated
  - 1 | additional egg white
  - ¼ tsp | cream of tartar
  - 1½ cups | grated Gruyère
  - ¼ tsp | cayenne pepper
  - ¼ tsp | freshly grated nutmeg
  - ½ tsp | salt
method:
  - Heat the oven to 400°F. Butter four 8-oz ramekins, then coat the inside with Parmesan, tapping out the excess.
  - Melt the butter in a saucepan over medium heat, whisk in the flour, and cook until it smells nutty and no longer raw, about 1 minute.
  - Whisk in the warm milk and cook, stirring constantly, until the béchamel is thick enough to coat the back of a spoon.
  - Off the heat, whisk in the egg yolks one at a time, then stir in the Gruyère, cayenne, nutmeg, and salt until the cheese melts into the base.
  - Beat the egg whites and cream of tartar to stiff, glossy peaks.
  - Fold a third of the whites into the cheese base to loosen it, then fold in the rest until no white streaks remain.
  - Divide the batter among the ramekins and run a thumb around the inside rim of each to help the rise.
  - Bake until puffed well above the rim and deeply golden on top, about 20 minutes. Do not open the oven before then.
  - Serve immediately, before the soufflés have a chance to fall.
---

The rise comes entirely from the whipped whites, so treat them with more care than the base: stiff, glossy peaks, folded in gently rather than stirred. Overmix at that stage and the soufflé bakes up short no matter how hot the oven is.

This one doesn't wait — there's no make-ahead version, since the structure that gets it out of the ramekin is also what collapses within minutes of leaving the oven. Cheddar or a sharp blue cheese both work in place of the Gruyère if you want something more assertive; serve with something acidic alongside, like a green salad in a sharp vinaigrette, to cut the richness.
