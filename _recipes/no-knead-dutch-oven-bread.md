---
title: No-Knead Dutch Oven Bread
date: 2026-07-27 18:45:41 -0400
description: A crusty round loaf built on an overnight rise instead of a mixer, baked covered so the crust blisters on its own steam.
image: /assets/images/No-Knead-Dutch-Oven-Bread.webp
servings: 8
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 50 min
tags: [bread, baking, vegetarian, slow, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 3 cups | bread flour
  - 1½ tsp | salt
  - ¼ tsp | active dry yeast
  - 1½ cups | warm water
  - To finish | cornmeal, for dusting
method:
  - Whisk the flour, salt, and yeast together in a large bowl.
  - Stir in the warm water until no dry flour remains and the dough is shaggy and wet.
  - Cover the bowl and leave at room temperature for 12 to 18 hours, until the surface is dotted with bubbles and the dough has more than doubled.
  - Turn the dough onto a well-floured surface and fold it over itself a few times into a rough ball.
  - Set the ball seam-side down on a cornmeal-dusted towel, dust the top, and let rise for 1 to 2 hours, until it holds a dent when poked.
  - Thirty minutes before baking, set a covered Dutch oven inside and heat the oven to 450°F.
  - Lift the dough into the hot pot, score the top with a sharp knife, and cover.
  - Bake covered for 30 minutes, then uncover and bake 15 to 20 minutes more, until the crust is deep brown and the loaf sounds hollow when tapped on the bottom.
  - Cool on a rack for at least an hour before slicing.
---

The overnight rise does the work a stand mixer would otherwise do — left alone that long, the gluten develops on its own, so the only real skill this asks for is patience and a pot with a lid. A cast-iron or enameled Dutch oven both work; what matters is that it holds steam around the loaf for the first half of the bake.

Slice it too soon and the crumb turns gummy under a beautiful crust — let it cool fully, even though it's hard to wait. Stale a day later, it's better toasted than fresh.
