title: Eggs Benedict with Classic Hollandaise
date: 2026-07-31 00:00:02 -0400
description: A poached egg and warm butter sauce doing all the work — the only real skill is timing three things to land at once.
image: /assets/images/Eggs-Benedict-With-Classic-Hollandaise.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 20 min
tags: [eggs, breakfast]
ingredients:
  - 4 | English muffins, split
  - 8 | eggs
  - 8 slices | Canadian bacon or ham
  - 3 | egg yolks
  - 1 cup | unsalted butter, melted
  - 1 tbsp | lemon juice
  - 1 tbsp | white vinegar
  - To taste | cayenne pepper
  - To taste | kosher salt
  - To finish | chives, chopped
method:
  - Bring a wide, deep skillet of water to a bare simmer and stir in the white vinegar.
  - Melt the butter in a small saucepan and keep it warm without letting it brown.
  - Set a bowl over a pot of barely simmering water and whisk the egg yolks with the lemon juice until pale, thick, and doubled in volume.
  - Off the heat, drizzle in the warm butter a few drops at a time, whisking constantly, until the sauce turns glossy and holds a ribbon; season with cayenne and salt and set it somewhere warm, not hot.
  - Crack each egg into a small cup, swirl the simmering water into a gentle whirlpool, and slide the egg in; poach 3 minutes, until the white is fully set and the yolk still wobbles.
  - Lift each egg out with a slotted spoon and rest it on a paper towel while you finish the rest.
  - Toast the muffin halves and sear the Canadian bacon in a hot dry pan until the edges color, about 2 minutes.
  - Stack muffin, bacon, and a poached egg, spoon the hollandaise over the top, and finish with chives.
---

Hollandaise is just an emulsion, and it only breaks two ways: butter added too fast, or the bowl gone too hot. If it splits into puddles of fat, pull it off the heat, whisk in a teaspoon of hot water, and it usually comes back together. If it doesn't, start a fresh yolk in a clean bowl and drizzle the broken sauce in the same way you did the butter.

Poach the eggs up to a few hours ahead and hold them in a bowl of ice water — a 30-second dunk back in the simmering water at assembly time reheats them without pushing the yolk past soft. Canadian bacon is the classic, but a handful of wilted spinach or a few folds of smoked salmon underneath works just as well.
