---
title: Halibut en Papillote with Cherry Tomatoes and Basil
date: 2026-07-30 20:00:03 -0400
description: Fish and tomatoes sealed in parchment and baked into their own sauce, so the packet does the work a saucepan usually would.
image: /assets/images/Halibut-En-Papillote-With-Cherry-Tomatoes-And-Basil.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 15 min
tags: [fish, baking, quick, main]
ingredients:
  - 4 (6 oz) | halibut fillets
  - 1 pint | cherry tomatoes, halved
  - 2 tbsp | olive oil
  - 3 cloves | garlic, thinly sliced
  - ¼ cup | dry white wine
  - 1 | lemon, thinly sliced
  - A handful | basil leaves
  - Kosher salt and black pepper, to taste
method:
  - Cut four 15-inch squares of parchment paper and fold each in half to crease, then open flat again.
  - Toss the cherry tomatoes with the olive oil, garlic, salt, and pepper.
  - Season the halibut and set one fillet on one half of each parchment square, then top with the tomato mixture, a few lemon slices, and a splash of white wine.
  - Fold the parchment over and crimp the edges in tight, overlapping folds until each packet is fully sealed.
  - Bake at 400°F until the packets are puffed and taut, about 15 minutes.
  - Tear each packet open at the table, angling it away from your face — the steam that rushes out is the point, but it's scalding.
---

The seal is what makes this work: if steam can escape, the fish steams in air instead of in tomato-and-wine liquid, and it dries out before it cooks through. Crimp the parchment like you mean it, and don't peek early to check — every time you open a packet, you let out the thing that's cooking the fish.

Halibut holds its shape best, but any firm white fillet — cod, snapper, sea bass — works the same way. Serve the packets unopened and let people tear into their own; the smell that comes off them is half the dish.
