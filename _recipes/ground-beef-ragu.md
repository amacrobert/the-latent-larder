---
title: 30-Minute Ground Beef Ragù with Rigatoni
date: 2026-07-31 08:43:43 -0400
description: A weeknight ragù that skips the all-day simmer — browned beef, crushed tomatoes, and just enough wine to matter.
image: /assets/images/Ground-Beef-Ragu.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 10 min
cook_time: 20 min
tags: [beef, pasta, quick, main]
ingredients:
  - 1 lb | ground beef
  - 1 tbsp | olive oil
  - 1 | yellow onion, finely chopped
  - 3 cloves | garlic, minced
  - 2 tbsp | tomato paste
  - 28 oz | canned crushed tomatoes
  - ½ cup | dry red wine
  - 1 tsp | dried oregano
  - ½ tsp | red pepper flakes
  - 12 oz | rigatoni
  - ½ cup | grated Parmesan, plus more to finish
  - To finish | torn basil
method:
  - Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil for the pasta.
  - Heat the olive oil in a large skillet or Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Add the onion and cook until softened and turning translucent, about 4 minutes.
  - Add the garlic and cook until fragrant, about 30 seconds.
  - Add the ground beef, breaking it apart with a spoon, and cook until no pink remains and the edges have started to brown.
  - Stir in the tomato paste and cook until it darkens slightly and coats the meat.
  - Pour in the wine, scraping up any browned bits from the pan, and simmer until mostly evaporated.
  - Add the crushed tomatoes, oregano, and red pepper flakes. Simmer, stirring occasionally, until the sauce has thickened and no longer tastes sharply of raw tomato, about 15 minutes.
  - Meanwhile, cook the rigatoni in the boiling water until al dente. Reserve ½ cup of the pasta water, then drain.
  - Add the drained pasta and Parmesan to the sauce, along with a splash of the reserved pasta water, and toss until glossy and every piece is coated.
  - Divide among bowls and top with more Parmesan and torn basil.
---

The tomato paste step is the one people skip and shouldn't — a minute or two cooking it down before the tomatoes go in is most of what separates this from sauce out of a jar. Don't rush past it just because the pot looks ready for liquid.

No red wine open? A splash of balsamic vinegar off the heat at the end gets you most of the same lift. Ground pork or a beef-pork mix works here too, if that's what's in the fridge.
