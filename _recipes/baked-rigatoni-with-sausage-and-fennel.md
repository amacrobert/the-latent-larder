---
title: Baked Rigatoni with Sausage and Fennel
date: 2026-07-26
description: A one-pot pasta bake that finishes under the broiler, built for a table that needs feeding twice.
image: /assets/images/Baked-Rigatoni-With-Sausage-And-Fennel.webp
servings: 6
prep_time: 20 min
cook_time: 45 min
tags: [pasta, main, baking]
ingredients:
  - 1 lb | rigatoni
  - 1 lb | Italian sausage, casings removed
  - 2 tbsp | olive oil
  - 1 | fennel bulb, thinly sliced
  - 1 | onion, diced
  - 3 cloves | garlic, minced
  - ½ cup | dry white wine
  - 28 oz | canned crushed tomatoes
  - ½ tsp | red pepper flakes
  - 8 oz | mozzarella, torn
  - ½ cup | grated Parmesan
  - To finish | fennel fronds or parsley
method:
  - Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the rigatoni about 2 minutes short of the package's al dente time, then drain.
  - Heat the olive oil in a wide, oven-safe pot over medium-high and brown the sausage, breaking it into pieces, until no pink remains.
  - Add the fennel and onion and cook until the fennel has softened and turned translucent at the edges, about 8 minutes.
  - Stir in the garlic and red pepper flakes and cook until fragrant, about 30 seconds.
  - Pour in the wine, scraping up the browned bits, and simmer until it has mostly evaporated.
  - Add the crushed tomatoes and simmer until slightly thickened, about 10 minutes.
  - Fold the drained rigatoni into the sauce along with half the mozzarella, then scatter the rest of the mozzarella and the Parmesan over the top.
  - Bake at 425°F until the cheese is browned in patches and the sauce bubbles at the edges, about 20 minutes.
  - Rest for 5 minutes, scatter with fennel fronds, and serve straight from the pot.
---

Pull the pasta before it's done — it keeps cooking in the oven, and rigatoni that was already al dente at the stove turns to paste by the time the cheese browns. Cooking everything in one oven-safe pot means no scraping sauce into a separate dish, and one less thing to wash.

No fennel bulb, no problem: a diced celery rib and a pinch of fennel seed gets you most of the way there. For a vegetarian version, swap the sausage for a can of drained white beans added with the tomatoes, and use a good pinch of extra red pepper flakes to make up for what the sausage was doing.
