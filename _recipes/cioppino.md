---
title: Cioppino
date: 2026-07-27 02:45:10 -0400
description: A tomato-and-wine seafood stew built to hold whatever's freshest at the fish counter, mopped up with torn bread.
image: /assets/images/Cioppino.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 25 min
cook_time: 45 min
tags: [shellfish, fish, soup, slow, main]
ingredients:
  - ¼ cup | olive oil
  - 1 | onion, diced
  - 1 | fennel bulb, diced
  - 4 cloves | garlic, minced
  - ¼ tsp | red pepper flakes
  - 1 cup | dry white wine
  - 28 oz | canned crushed tomatoes
  - 2 cups | fish stock or clam juice
  - 2 | bay leaves
  - 1 lb | firm white fish, cut into chunks
  - 1 lb | clams, scrubbed
  - ½ lb | shrimp, peeled and deveined
  - To finish | parsley and lemon wedges
method:
  - Heat the olive oil in a Dutch oven over medium heat and cook the onion and fennel until soft and translucent, about 10 minutes.
  - Add the garlic and red pepper flakes and cook until fragrant, about 1 minute.
  - Pour in the wine and simmer until reduced by half, about 5 minutes.
  - Stir in the tomatoes, fish stock, and bay leaves, bring to a simmer, and cook uncovered until slightly thickened, about 20 minutes.
  - Nestle the clams into the broth, cover, and cook until they begin to open, about 5 minutes.
  - Add the fish and shrimp, cover again, and cook until the fish is opaque and the shrimp are pink, about 5 minutes.
  - Discard the bay leaves and any clams that haven't opened.
  - Ladle into bowls, scatter with parsley, and serve with lemon wedges.
---

Build the base first and don't rush the reduction — a broth simmered down before the seafood goes in tastes like it's been on the stove all afternoon, even though the shellfish itself only needs ten minutes. Whatever's freshest wins here: swap in mussels, crab legs, or scallops for any of the three, keeping the total weight about the same.

This wants a crusty loaf on the side for the broth, not rice or pasta underneath it. Leftover broth (without the seafood still in it) freezes well for a faster version next time.
