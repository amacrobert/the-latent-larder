---
title: Key Lime Pie with Toasted Meringue
date: 2026-08-03 16:00:04 -0400
description: A tart condensed-milk custard in a graham crust, baked just long enough to set, then browned under a broiler instead of buried in whipped cream.
image: /assets/images/Key-Lime-Pie-With-Toasted-Meringue.webp
servings: 8
prep_time: 20 min
cook_time: 20 min
tags: [dessert, baking, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 1½ cups | graham cracker crumbs
  - 6 tbsp | unsalted butter, melted
  - 3 tbsp | granulated sugar, for the crust
  - 4 | egg yolks
  - 14 oz | sweetened condensed milk
  - ½ cup | key lime juice (or regular lime juice)
  - 1 tbsp | lime zest
  - 4 | egg whites
  - ¼ tsp | cream of tartar
  - 6 tbsp | granulated sugar, for the meringue
method:
  - Heat the oven to 350°F.
  - Stir the graham cracker crumbs, melted butter, and 3 tbsp sugar until every crumb is damp, then press firmly into a 9-inch pie plate, up the sides and across the bottom.
  - Bake the crust until the edges have darkened slightly, about 10 minutes, then cool while you make the filling.
  - Whisk the egg yolks until pale, then whisk in the condensed milk, lime juice, and zest until smooth and slightly thickened.
  - Pour the filling into the warm crust and bake until the center is set but still gives a slight wobble when nudged, 12 to 15 minutes.
  - Cool to room temperature, then refrigerate uncovered at least 3 hours, until fully chilled.
  - Beat the egg whites and cream of tartar to soft peaks, then add the meringue sugar a spoonful at a time and beat to stiff, glossy peaks.
  - Swirl the meringue over the chilled pie right to the crust edge, sealing it against the graham rim, then broil until the peaks are browned in spots, 1 to 2 minutes, watching the whole time.
---

Real key limes are more floral and less sharp than the bottled juice most stores stock, but bottled key lime juice still beats regular limes, which taste flatter here — either works, and it's the zest that carries most of the perfume.

Seal the meringue to the crust before it goes under the broiler; a gap at the edge is where it shrinks and weeps as it cools. If you'd rather skip the broiler step, whipped cream added just before serving is the classic substitute.
