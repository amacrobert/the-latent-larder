---
title: Steamed Whole Fish with Ginger and Scallion
date: 2026-07-30 00:30:02 -0400
description: A whole fish steamed until just opaque, then hit with a hissing pour of hot oil over ginger and scallion — dinner in twenty minutes with no oven at all.
image: /assets/images/Steamed-Whole-Fish-With-Ginger-And-Scallion.webp
servings: 2
prep_time: 10 min
cook_time: 12 min
tags: [fish, steamed, quick, main]
ingredients:
  - 1 whole | branzino or sea bass (1 to 1½ lb), cleaned and scaled
  - 2 tbsp | shaoxing wine or dry sherry
  - 2 in | ginger, half julienned, half sliced
  - 4 | scallions, split lengthwise into thin strips
  - 3 tbsp | soy sauce
  - 1 tsp | sugar
  - 3 tbsp | neutral oil
  - A handful | cilantro leaves
method:
  - Score the fish two or three times on each side, then rub it inside and out with the wine and sliced ginger.
  - Lay the fish on a heatproof plate that fits inside your steamer or a wok fitted with a rack, and bring an inch of water to a rolling boil underneath.
  - Steam covered until the flesh at the thickest point turns opaque and flakes from the bone, 10 to 12 minutes for a 1½-pound fish.
  - While the fish steams, warm the soy sauce and sugar in a small pot just until the sugar dissolves, then set aside.
  - Lift the fish onto a serving platter, pour off any liquid that's pooled on the plate, and scatter the julienned ginger and scallions over the top.
  - Heat the oil in a small pan until it's shimmering and just short of smoking, then pour it directly over the scallions and ginger so it sizzles loudly on contact.
  - Pour the warm soy mixture around the fish, scatter with cilantro, and serve immediately.
---

The hot oil is the whole trick — poured over raw aromatics, it cooks them in place and turns the scallion from sharp to sweet in about two seconds, which is a more dramatic effect than anything the fish itself is doing. Don't pour the soy sauce over first; it'll mute the sizzle and the oil won't get the same lift.

Any thin, mild whole fish works here — sea bass, snapper, or tilapia all steam in roughly the same time. Check for doneness early rather than late, since a fish that's overcooked by even a minute goes from silky to chalky, and there's no browning to hide behind.
