---
title: Buttermilk Panna Cotta with Roasted Cherries
date: 2026-07-25 22:45:25 -0400
description: A barely-set dessert with enough tang to stop it tasting like sweetened cream.
image: /assets/images/Buttermilk-Panna-Cotta-With-Roasted-Cherries.webp
servings: 6
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 20 min
tags: [dessert, fruit, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 1½ cups | heavy cream
  - ½ cup | granulated sugar
  - 1 envelope | powdered gelatin
  - 3 tbsp | cold water
  - 1¾ cups | buttermilk
  - 1 tsp | vanilla extract
  - 1 lb | cherries, pitted
  - 2 tbsp | granulated sugar
  - 1 tbsp | lemon juice
  - Pinch | salt
method:
  - Sprinkle the gelatin over the cold water in a small bowl and leave it until softened, about 5 minutes.
  - Warm the cream and ½ cup sugar in a saucepan over medium heat, stirring, until the sugar dissolves and steam just starts to rise — don't let it boil.
  - Off the heat, whisk in the softened gelatin until it's fully dissolved and no grains remain.
  - Whisk in the buttermilk and vanilla, then strain through a sieve into a jug for a completely smooth set.
  - Divide between six glasses and refrigerate uncovered until fully set, at least 4 hours.
  - Heat the oven to 400°F. Toss the cherries with the remaining sugar, lemon juice, and salt on a sheet pan.
  - Roast until the cherries have collapsed and released their juices, about 20 minutes, then cool to room temperature.
  - Spoon the cherries and their juice over the panna cotta just before serving.
---

The buttermilk is doing the real work here — swap it for more cream and you've
just made sweetened jelly. Don't skip straining the base either: it's the only
thing standing between a silky set and one with flecks of unmelted gelatin
running through it.

Frozen cherries roast just as well as fresh if it's not the season for them,
though you'll lose a minute or two off the roasting time. Any leftover fruit
syrup is worth keeping for stirring into soda water.
