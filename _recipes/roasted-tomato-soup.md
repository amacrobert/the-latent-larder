---
title: Roasted Tomato Soup with Basil
date: 2026-08-05 17:00:03 -0400
description: Plum tomatoes roasted until charred and jammy, then blended with broth and cream — the classic dunking partner for a grilled cheese.
image: /assets/images/Roasted-Tomato-Soup.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 45 min
tags: [soup, vegetarian, roasted, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 3 lb | plum tomatoes, halved
  - 1 | yellow onion, quartered
  - 6 cloves | garlic, unpeeled
  - ¼ cup | olive oil
  - 2 tbsp | tomato paste
  - 3 cups | vegetable stock
  - ½ cup | heavy cream
  - ¼ cup | fresh basil leaves, torn
  - 2 tbsp | unsalted butter
  - Salt and pepper, to taste
  - To finish | grated Parmesan
method:
  - Heat the oven to 425°F. Toss the tomatoes, onion, and garlic with the olive oil, salt, and pepper on a sheet pan.
  - Roast until the tomatoes have collapsed and the skins are charred in spots, about 35 minutes.
  - Squeeze the roasted garlic cloves out of their skins and discard the skins.
  - Melt the butter in a soup pot over medium heat, stir in the tomato paste, and cook until it darkens a shade, about 1 minute.
  - Scrape in the roasted tomatoes, onion, and garlic along with the stock, and bring to a simmer.
  - Simmer for 10 minutes, then puree with an immersion blender until completely smooth.
  - Stir in the cream and basil, and season with salt and pepper.
  - Ladle into bowls and finish with grated Parmesan.
---

Roasting the tomatoes first is what separates this from the can — 35 minutes in a hot oven drives off water and turns the sugars, so the soup comes out sweeter and less sharp without a spoonful of sugar to fix it. Canned whole tomatoes work in winter; just skip straight to the pot and simmer them longer to make up for the roast you're skipping.

Blend it as smooth as your machine allows — tomato skins that don't fully break down turn up as flecks, which is fine in a rustic soup but not what you want here. Ladle it next to a [grilled cheese](/recipes/grilled-cheese-with-caramelized-onions/) for dipping and call it dinner.
