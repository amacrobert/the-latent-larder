---
title: Pumpkin Pie with a Gingersnap Crust
date: 2026-08-01 06:30:03 -0400
description: A custard pie in a spiced cookie-crumb shell, baked just until the center still holds a slow wobble.
image: /assets/images/Pumpkin-Pie-With-A-Gingersnap-Crust.webp
servings: 8
prep_time: 20 min
cook_time: 55 min
tags: [dessert, baking, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 2 cups | gingersnap cookies, finely crushed
  - 6 tbsp | butter, melted
  - 2 tbsp | brown sugar
  - 15 oz | pumpkin purée
  - ¾ cup | heavy cream
  - ¾ cup | brown sugar
  - 3 | eggs
  - 1 tsp | cinnamon
  - ½ tsp | ginger
  - ¼ tsp | cloves
  - ¼ tsp | nutmeg
  - ½ tsp | salt
  - To finish | whipped cream
method:
  - Heat the oven to 350°F.
  - Stir the crushed gingersnaps, melted butter, and brown sugar until every crumb is damp, then press firmly into a 9-inch pie plate, up the sides and across the bottom.
  - Bake the crust until it smells toasted and holds its shape when pressed, about 10 minutes, then cool slightly.
  - Whisk the pumpkin purée, cream, brown sugar, eggs, cinnamon, ginger, cloves, nutmeg, and salt until fully smooth.
  - Pour the filling into the warm crust.
  - Bake until the edges are set and puffed but the center still jiggles like loose gelatin when the pan is nudged, 40 to 45 minutes.
  - Cool on a rack to room temperature, then refrigerate uncovered at least 3 hours, until fully set.
---

The crust bakes first and alone — a raw gingersnap shell under a wet custard turns soggy before the filling ever sets, and the ten-minute head start is what keeps it crisp underneath.

Pull the pie while the center still moves as a single slow wave, not ripples — it firms up entirely in the fridge, and a center baked solid in the oven is already headed for cracks. Canned pumpkin purée works better here than fresh-roasted squash, which carries too much water and thins the set.
