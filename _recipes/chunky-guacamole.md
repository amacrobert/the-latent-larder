---
title: Chunky Guacamole with Lime and Jalapeño
date: 2026-07-31 20:30:03 -0400
description: Mashed by hand and dressed with enough lime and salt that it never needs a garnish.
image: /assets/images/Chunky-Guacamole-With-Lime-And-Jalapeño.webp
servings: 6
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 0 min
tags: [appetizer, dip, vegetarian, quick]
ingredients:
  - 4 | ripe avocados
  - 2 | limes, juiced
  - ¼ cup | red onion, finely diced
  - 1 | jalapeño, seeded and minced
  - ¼ cup | cilantro, chopped
  - 1 clove | garlic, grated
  - 1 tsp | kosher salt, more to taste
  - 1 | Roma tomato, seeded and diced (optional)
  - Tortilla chips, to serve
method:
  - Halve and pit the avocados, then scoop the flesh into a wide bowl.
  - Mash with a fork until mostly smooth with streaks of chunk still visible — not a purée.
  - Fold in the lime juice, onion, jalapeño, cilantro, and garlic.
  - Season with the salt and taste, adding more lime or salt until the flavor is sharp rather than flat.
  - Fold in the tomato just before serving, if using.
---

The lime juice is doing two jobs, not one — it seasons, and the acid slows the browning, so don't skimp on it if the bowl needs to sit for a bit before it hits the table. If it does have to wait, press plastic wrap directly onto the surface rather than laying it over the bowl; keeping air off the avocado matters more than the fridge does.

Mash by hand, not in a food processor — guacamole should still look like something that started as fruit. It's the natural second act to a night of [tacos](/recipes/crispy-ground-beef-tacos-with-pickled-onion/) or a bowl of [chili](/recipes/slow-cooker-ground-beef-chili/), and it disappears just as fast on its own with a bag of chips.
