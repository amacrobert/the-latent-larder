---
title: Roasted Bone Marrow with Parsley Salad
date: 2026-07-31 07:30:02 -0400
description: Halved marrow bones roasted until the centers turn to warm custard, spooned onto toast under a sharp, mustardy parsley salad.
image: /assets/images/Roasted-Bone-Marrow-With-Parsley-Salad.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 10 min
cook_time: 20 min
tags: [beef, appetizer, quick]
ingredients:
  - 4 (6-inch) | marrow bones, split lengthwise by the butcher
  - 1 bunch | flat-leaf parsley, leaves picked
  - 1 small | shallot, thinly sliced
  - 2 tbsp | capers, drained and roughly chopped
  - 1 tbsp | sherry vinegar
  - 2 tbsp | olive oil
  - 1 tsp | Dijon mustard
  - 8 slices | baguette, sliced on the bias
  - Flaky salt, to finish
method:
  - Heat the oven to 450°F and arrange the marrow bones cut-side up on a rimmed baking sheet.
  - Season the marrow generously with salt and roast until it's puffed and jiggles like custard at the center but hasn't fully melted away, 15 to 20 minutes.
  - While the bones roast, toss the parsley, shallot, and capers in a bowl.
  - Whisk the vinegar, olive oil, and mustard together, season with salt, and dress the parsley salad just before serving so it doesn't wilt.
  - Toast the baguette slices until golden at the edges.
  - Set the bones on a platter, top each with a small pile of the parsley salad, and finish with flaky salt.
  - Serve immediately with the toast alongside, for spreading the marrow and scooping up whatever falls off the bone.
---

Marrow keeps roasting after the salad hits it, so time the two together — pull the bones the moment they jiggle, since a few extra minutes renders them from custard to oil and there's nothing left to spread. Ask the butcher to cut the bones lengthwise (canoe-style) rather than into rounds; it's far easier to get a spoon in.

The parsley salad isn't garnish, it's the point — straight marrow is rich enough to turn on you by the third bite, and the vinegar and capers are what keep you reaching for a fourth. A glass of something with real acid, a cru Beaujolais or a lean Sancerre, does the same job for the wine.
