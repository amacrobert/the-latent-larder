---
title: Chewy Granola Bars with Honey and Almonds
date: 2026-08-07 00:00:03 -0400
description: Toasted oats pressed into a honey-almond binder and baked once, sturdy enough to survive a lunchbox without crumbling.
image: /assets/images/Chewy-Granola-Bars-With-Honey-And-Almonds.webp
servings: 16 bars
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 25 min
tags: [snack, breakfast, baking, vegetarian, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 3 cups | old-fashioned rolled oats
  - 1 cup | sliced almonds
  - ½ cup | honey
  - ¼ cup | packed brown sugar
  - ¼ cup | unsalted butter
  - ½ cup | creamy almond butter
  - 1 tsp | vanilla extract
  - ½ tsp | kosher salt
  - ½ cup | mini chocolate chips
method:
  - Preheat the oven to 325°F. Line a 9x13-inch pan with parchment, leaving overhang on the two long sides.
  - Spread the oats and almonds on a rimmed baking sheet and toast until fragrant and lightly golden, about 8 minutes.
  - Meanwhile, combine the honey, brown sugar, and butter in a small saucepan over medium heat and cook, stirring, until the butter melts and the mixture bubbles at the edges.
  - Off heat, whisk in the almond butter, vanilla, and salt until smooth.
  - In a large bowl, stir the toasted oats and almonds into the honey mixture until every oat is coated.
  - Let cool for 5 minutes, then fold in the chocolate chips.
  - Press the mixture firmly and evenly into the prepared pan — lean on it hard, since a loosely packed bar falls apart when sliced.
  - Bake until the edges are deep golden brown, 12 to 15 minutes.
  - Cool completely in the pan on a wire rack, at least 2 hours, before lifting out by the parchment and slicing into 16 bars.
---

The cooling is the recipe. Slice these while they're still warm and they'll fall apart into granola; give them the full two hours (or move the pan to the fridge to speed it along) and the honey sets up enough to hold a clean cut edge.

For a nut-free version, swap the almond butter for sunflower seed butter and the sliced almonds for pepitas or extra oats — the ratio of binder to dry mix is what matters, not which nuts get toasted into it.
