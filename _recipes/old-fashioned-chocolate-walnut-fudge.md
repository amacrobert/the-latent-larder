---
title: Old-Fashioned Chocolate Walnut Fudge
date: 2026-08-12 06:00:03 -0400
description: Real cooked fudge — sugar syrup taken to the soft-ball stage, cooled untouched, then beaten by hand until it turns matte and sets firm.
image: /assets/images/Old-Fashioned-Chocolate-Walnut-Fudge.webp
servings: 32 pieces
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 20 min
tags: [dessert, make-ahead, vegetarian]
ingredients:
  - 3 cups | granulated sugar
  - ¾ cup | unsalted butter
  - 1 (5 oz) can | evaporated milk
  - 4 oz | unsweetened chocolate, chopped
  - ¼ tsp | salt
  - 1 tbsp | vanilla extract
  - 1 cup | walnuts, chopped
method:
  - Butter a 9x9-inch pan and line it with parchment, leaving an overhang on two sides.
  - Combine the sugar, butter, evaporated milk, chocolate, and salt in a heavy saucepan over medium heat, stirring until the chocolate melts and the sugar dissolves.
  - Clip on a candy thermometer and boil, stirring only occasionally, until it reaches 238°F, the soft-ball stage.
  - Pour the mixture onto a marble slab or into a wide heatproof bowl without scraping the pan, and let it cool undisturbed until just warm to the touch, about 45 minutes.
  - Add the vanilla, then beat with a wooden spoon until the fudge turns thick, matte, and just barely holds its shape when dropped from the spoon, 8 to 10 minutes.
  - Fold in the walnuts quickly, once the fudge starts to stiffen, and press into the prepared pan before it sets solid.
  - Cool completely at room temperature, at least 2 hours, before lifting out and cutting into squares.
---

The thermometer gets you to the right sugar concentration, but the beating is what actually makes fudge instead of candy glass — it forces the sugar to crystallize fine and even, which is the difference between a smooth square and a grainy one. Don't scrape the pan when you pour, and don't stir while it cools; disturbing it before it's ready seeds early, uneven crystals that never smooth back out.

This is the pan-and-spoon method, not the condensed-milk shortcut — it takes an hour of mostly waiting, but nothing here needs a mixer. Toast the walnuts first if you want more crunch against the soft set, or leave them out for plain chocolate fudge.
