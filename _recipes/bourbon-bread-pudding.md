---
title: New Orleans-Style Bread Pudding with Bourbon Sauce
date: 2026-08-11 18:00:02 -0400
description: Stale challah baked into a barely-set custard, then soaked in a butter-and-bourbon sauce right before it hits the table.
image: /assets/images/Bourbon-Bread-Pudding.webp
servings: 8
prep_time: 20 min
cook_time: 50 min
tags: [dessert, baking, bread, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 8 cups | day-old challah or brioche, torn into 1-inch pieces
  - 2 cups | whole milk
  - 1 cup | heavy cream
  - 4 | eggs
  - ¾ cup | granulated sugar
  - 2 tsp | vanilla extract
  - 1 tsp | cinnamon
  - ¼ tsp | nutmeg
  - ¼ tsp | salt
  - ½ cup | raisins
  - ½ cup | unsalted butter
  - ½ cup | packed brown sugar
  - ¼ cup | heavy cream
  - ¼ cup | bourbon
  - Pinch | salt
method:
  - Preheat the oven to 350°F and butter a 9x13-inch baking dish.
  - Spread the torn bread in the dish and scatter the raisins over top.
  - Whisk the milk, 1 cup cream, eggs, granulated sugar, vanilla, cinnamon, nutmeg, and ¼ tsp salt together until smooth.
  - Pour the custard evenly over the bread and press down gently so every piece is soaked.
  - Let it sit 15 minutes so the bread fully absorbs the custard before it goes in the oven.
  - Bake until puffed and set at the edges but still faintly jiggly in the center, 45-50 minutes.
  - While the pudding bakes, melt the butter and brown sugar in a small saucepan over medium heat, whisking until the sugar dissolves, about 2 minutes.
  - Off the heat, whisk in ¼ cup cream, the bourbon, and a pinch of salt until the sauce is smooth.
  - Let the pudding cool 10 minutes, then pour the warm bourbon sauce over the top before serving.
---

Fresh bread turns to paste here — the whole point of using something a day or two stale is that it holds its shape while it drinks up the custard instead of dissolving into it. If all you've got is a fresh loaf, cube it and leave it out on the counter for a few hours first.

Raisins are traditional but not load-bearing; toasted pecans or a handful of chopped dried cherries work just as well, or leave the pudding plain and let the sauce carry it. For a version without the bourbon, swap in the same amount of cream and a splash more vanilla — still worth making, just a different dessert.
