---
title: Chocolate Eclairs with Vanilla Pastry Cream
date: 2026-08-12 21:00:03 -0400
description: Piped choux batons filled with real stovetop pastry cream and dipped in a dark chocolate glaze, not the ice-cream-and-fudge version.
image: /assets/images/Chocolate-Eclairs-With-Vanilla-Pastry-Cream.webp
servings: 6
prep_time: 40 min
cook_time: 35 min
tags: [dessert, baking, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 2 cups | whole milk
  - 6 | large egg yolks
  - ½ cup | sugar
  - ¼ cup | cornstarch
  - Pinch | salt
  - 2 tsp | vanilla extract
  - 2 tbsp | unsalted butter, for the cream
  - 1 cup | water
  - ½ cup | unsalted butter, for the dough
  - 1 tbsp | sugar, for the dough
  - ¼ tsp | salt, for the dough
  - 1 cup | all-purpose flour
  - 4 | large eggs
  - 4 oz | bittersweet chocolate, chopped
  - ½ cup | heavy cream, for the glaze
  - 1 tbsp | light corn syrup
method:
  - Whisk the egg yolks, sugar, cornstarch, and a pinch of salt in a bowl until pale and no lumps remain.
  - Bring the milk to a bare simmer, then pour it slowly into the yolk mixture while whisking constantly so the eggs don't scramble.
  - Return everything to the saucepan and cook over medium heat, whisking hard, until the cream thickens to a pudding-like consistency and holds a bubble for a few seconds — about 2 minutes past the first bubble.
  - Off the heat, whisk in the vanilla and 2 tablespoons butter, then press plastic wrap directly onto the surface and chill at least 2 hours, until fully set.
  - Bring the water, ½ cup butter, 1 tablespoon sugar, and ¼ teaspoon salt to a full boil in a saucepan.
  - Dump in the flour all at once and stir hard until the dough balls up and a thin film coats the bottom of the pan, about a minute.
  - Scrape the dough into a bowl and beat for a minute to cool it slightly, then beat in the eggs one at a time until fully absorbed — the dough should fall off the spoon in a slow, glossy ribbon.
  - Transfer to a piping bag fitted with a large round or star tip and pipe 4-inch batons onto a lined baking sheet, spaced well apart.
  - Bake at 425°F for 15 minutes, then lower the oven to 350°F and keep baking until the shells are deeply golden, firm, and sound hollow when tapped, 20 to 25 more minutes.
  - Pierce each end with a paring knife and let cool completely on a rack — this lets steam escape so they stay crisp instead of going soggy.
  - Warm the ½ cup heavy cream in a small saucepan until it just simmers, then pour over the chopped chocolate and corn syrup and let sit a minute before stirring into a smooth, glossy glaze.
  - Split the shells lengthwise, pipe in the chilled pastry cream, dip the tops in the warm glaze, and let set a few minutes before serving.
---

The pastry cream needs the full two hours in the fridge — pipe it in warm and it weeps through the choux and turns the shells soggy before anyone gets a bite. Make it a day ahead if that's easier; it holds fine under plastic.

Same choux dough as a batch of profiteroles, piped into batons instead of mounds and dipped rather than sauced. Filled shells only keep their crunch for a couple of hours, so fill close to serving and store any extra pastry cream and unfilled shells separately.
