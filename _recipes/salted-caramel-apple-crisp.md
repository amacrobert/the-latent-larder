---
title: Salted Caramel Apple Crisp
date: 2026-08-10 06:00:03 -0400
description: Tart baking apples under a pecan-oat crumble, finished with a stovetop caramel sauce poured on right before it hits the table.
image: /assets/images/Salted-Caramel-Apple-Crisp.webp
servings: 8
prep_time: 25 min
cook_time: 45 min
tags: [dessert, baking, fruit]
ingredients:
  - 3 lb | apples, peeled and sliced
  - ¼ cup | granulated sugar
  - 2 tbsp | all-purpose flour
  - 1 tsp | cinnamon
  - ¼ tsp | nutmeg
  - 1 | lemon, juiced
  - 1 cup | old-fashioned oats
  - ¾ cup | all-purpose flour
  - ½ cup | brown sugar, packed
  - ½ cup | pecans, chopped
  - ½ tsp | cinnamon
  - ½ tsp | salt
  - ½ cup | cold butter, cubed
  - ½ cup | brown sugar, packed
  - ¼ cup | butter
  - ¼ cup | heavy cream
  - ½ tsp | flaky salt
method:
  - Preheat the oven to 375°F and butter a 9x13-inch baking dish.
  - Toss the apples with the granulated sugar, 2 tbsp flour, cinnamon, nutmeg, and lemon juice, then spread them in the dish.
  - Rub the oats, ¾ cup flour, brown sugar, pecans, cinnamon, and salt together, working in the cold butter with your fingers until the mixture clumps into pea-to-marble-sized pieces.
  - Scatter the topping evenly over the apples without pressing it down.
  - Bake until the topping is deep golden and the fruit is bubbling thickly at the edges, 40-45 minutes.
  - While the crisp bakes, melt the butter and brown sugar in a small saucepan over medium heat, whisking until the sugar dissolves and the mixture bubbles, about 2 minutes.
  - Off the heat, whisk in the cream and flaky salt until the caramel is smooth.
  - Let the crisp rest 10 minutes, then drizzle the warm caramel over the top just before serving.
---

The caramel is what separates this from a plain apple crisp — it thins out over the warm topping and pools into the fruit underneath instead of sitting on top like a glaze. Make it while the crisp is still in the oven so both hit the table warm.

Mix a tart apple like Granny Smith with a sweeter one like Honeycrisp; all of one or the other makes a filling that's either sour enough to pucker or slack enough to weep. Good with vanilla ice cream, though the caramel does most of that job on its own.
