---
title: Creamy Stovetop Rice Pudding with Cinnamon
date: 2026-08-05 09:00:03 -0400
description: Rice simmered slowly in milk until it turns to a loose, spoonable custard — no oven, no gelatin, just a saucepan and patience.
image: /assets/images/Stovetop-Rice-Pudding.webp
servings: 6
prep_time: 5 min
cook_time: 45 min
tags: [dessert, rice, gluten-free, vegetarian, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - ½ cup | short-grain rice (arborio works well)
  - 3 cups | whole milk
  - ½ cup | granulated sugar
  - ¼ tsp | salt
  - 1 | cinnamon stick
  - 1 | egg yolk
  - ½ cup | heavy cream
  - 1 tsp | vanilla extract
  - ½ cup | raisins (optional)
  - To finish | ground cinnamon
method:
  - Combine the rice, milk, sugar, salt, and cinnamon stick in a heavy-bottomed saucepan and bring to a bare simmer over medium heat, stirring occasionally.
  - Reduce the heat to low and simmer, stirring often to keep the rice from catching on the bottom, until the rice is tender and the mixture has thickened to a loose porridge, 35-40 minutes.
  - Whisk the egg yolk and cream together in a small bowl, then whisk in a ladleful of the hot rice mixture to temper it.
  - Stir the tempered egg mixture back into the saucepan and cook over low heat, stirring constantly, until the pudding coats the back of a spoon, about 2 minutes — don't let it boil, or the yolk will curdle.
  - Remove the cinnamon stick, then stir in the vanilla and raisins if using.
  - Let cool for 10 minutes before serving — the pudding thickens further as it sits, and keeps thickening in the fridge.
---

Short-grain rice is doing real work here — its extra starch is what turns the milk into something thick and creamy rather than just rice floating in a thin liquid. Long-grain will still cook, but the pudding stays looser and never quite gets there.

Serve it warm the day it's made, or chill it and serve cold — both are right, and it thickens noticeably in the fridge, so loosen a stiff batch with a splash of milk before serving. Leave out the egg yolk and cream for a simpler, all-milk version; it won't be quite as rich, but it holds up just as well made a day ahead.
