---
title: Fermented Hot Sauce with Garlic and Lime
date: 2026-07-30 04:30:03 -0400
description: A chili mash left to sour on the counter for a week, then blended smooth — hotter and rounder than anything that comes from a bottle.
image: /assets/images/Fermented-Hot-Sauce-With-Garlic-And-Lime.webp
servings: 16
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 7 days (fermenting)
tags: [spicy, make-ahead, slow, vegetarian]
ingredients:
  - 1 lb | fresh red chilies (fresno or red jalapeño), stemmed
  - 6 cloves | garlic, peeled
  - 1 tbsp | kosher salt, plus more to taste
  - ¼ cup | distilled white vinegar
  - 2 tbsp | lime juice
method:
  - Pulse the chilies, garlic, and salt in a food processor until reduced to a coarse, wet mash.
  - Pack the mash into a clean quart jar, pressing down until the liquid rises above the solids, and leave an inch of headspace.
  - Cover loosely with a lid or cheesecloth and set somewhere warm and out of direct sun.
  - Press the mash back under its liquid once a day, loosening the lid to let gas escape, until it smells sharply sour and small bubbles rise when you press it down, 5 to 10 days.
  - Transfer the mash and its liquid to a blender, add the vinegar and lime juice, and blend until smooth.
  - Strain through a fine-mesh sieve, pressing on the solids to extract as much liquid as possible, and discard the pulp.
  - Taste, season with more salt if it needs it, then bottle and refrigerate.
---

The salt is doing more than seasoning: it pulls liquid out of the chilies to build the brine that keeps the mash submerged, starving it of the oxygen that mold needs. A thin white film on top is harmless kahm yeast and can be skimmed off, but anything fuzzy or colored means start over. Once bottled, the sauce keeps going a little softer and rounder in the fridge for months.

Any thin-walled fresh chili works here — swap in habaneros for something fruitier and considerably hotter, or mix in a few for heat without losing the fresno's color. It's built for eggs and fried rice, but it's just as good shaken into a pot of braised beans or stirred into mayonnaise for a sandwich.
