---
title: Slow Cooker Ground Beef Chili
date: 2026-07-31 09:30:03 -0400
description: Brown the meat, dump in the rest, and let the slow cooker run all day while you don't think about dinner.
image: /assets/images/Slow-Cooker-Ground-Beef-Chili.webp
servings: 6
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 7 hr
tags: [beef, main, slow, spicy]
ingredients:
  - 1 tbsp | olive oil
  - 2 lb | ground beef
  - 1 | yellow onion, diced
  - 1 | green bell pepper, diced
  - 4 cloves | garlic, minced
  - 3 tbsp | chili powder
  - 1 tbsp | ground cumin
  - 1 tsp | smoked paprika
  - ½ tsp | cayenne
  - 1 tbsp | tomato paste
  - 28 oz | canned crushed tomatoes
  - 2 cans | kidney beans (15 oz each), drained and rinsed
  - 1 cup | beef stock
  - To finish | shredded cheddar, sour cream, sliced scallions
method:
  - Heat the olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add the ground beef and cook, breaking it apart with a spoon, until no pink remains, about 8 minutes. Drain off excess fat.
  - Transfer the beef to a slow cooker. Add the onion, bell pepper, garlic, chili powder, cumin, smoked paprika, cayenne, and tomato paste, and stir until the meat is coated.
  - Add the crushed tomatoes, beans, and stock, and stir to combine.
  - Cover and cook on low until the vegetables have softened and the chili has thickened, 7 to 8 hours.
  - Stir well before serving — the chili firms up considerably as it sits, more than the pot suggests halfway through.
  - Ladle into bowls and top with cheddar, sour cream, and scallions.
---

Browning the beef before it goes in is the one step worth the extra skillet — a slow cooker never gets hot enough to brown anything, so any color the meat is going to pick up has to happen now or not at all. Everything else can go in raw.

This keeps for four or five days in the fridge and thickens further each time it's reheated, so it's worth making the full batch even for two people. Swap in pinto or black beans without changing anything else, and pass extra cayenne at the table rather than adding it to the pot — it's easier to add heat than take it away.
