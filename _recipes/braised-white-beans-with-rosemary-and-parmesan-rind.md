---
title: Braised White Beans with Rosemary and Parmesan Rind
date: 2026-07-31 01:30:02 -0400
description: Dried cannellini simmered low and slow with a parmesan rind until the broth turns creamy on its own, no cream required.
image: /assets/images/Braised-White-Beans-With-Rosemary-And-Parmesan-Rind.webp
servings: 6
prep_time: 15 min (plus overnight soak)
cook_time: 1 hr 45 min
tags: [beans, vegetarian, slow, side, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 1 lb | dried cannellini beans, soaked overnight
  - 1 piece | parmesan rind
  - 4 cloves | garlic, smashed
  - 1 sprig | rosemary
  - 1 | bay leaf
  - ¼ cup | olive oil, plus more to finish
  - To taste | kosher salt
  - To finish | lemon zest and parsley
method:
  - Drain and rinse the soaked beans, then set them in a heavy pot with the parmesan rind, garlic, rosemary, and bay leaf.
  - Cover with 2 inches of cold water, bring to a bare simmer, and skim off any foam that rises in the first few minutes.
  - Partially cover and simmer gently, topping up water as needed to keep the beans submerged, until they're tender all the way through and the broth has turned cloudy and thick, 1½ to 2 hours.
  - Stir in the olive oil and season with salt only once the beans are fully tender — salting the water early toughens the skins and slows the cook.
  - Fish out the rosemary sprig, bay leaf, and spent rind, then serve warm with more olive oil, lemon zest, and parsley over the top.
---

The parmesan rind is doing the work a ham hock would in a meatier version — save them in the freezer whenever you finish a wedge, because this is the reason to have one on hand. Don't rush the simmer by cranking the heat: a hard boil blows out the skins before the insides catch up, where a slow, barely-moving pot lets the starch loosen into its own sauce.

Good on its own with bread for the broth, or under a piece of roasted fish or seared sausage. It keeps better than it starts — a day in the fridge lets the beans finish soaking up the rosemary and garlic, so make it the day before if you can.
