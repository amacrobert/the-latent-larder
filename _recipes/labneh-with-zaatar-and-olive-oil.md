---
title: Labneh with Za'atar and Olive Oil
date: 2026-07-29 23:00:03 -0400
description: Yogurt salted and hung until the whey drips out on its own — a spread somewhere between yogurt and cheese, made without turning on the stove.
image: /assets/images/Labneh-With-Zaatar-And-Olive-Oil.webp
servings: 6
prep_time: 10 min (plus 24 hr draining)
cook_time: 0 min
tags: [vegetarian, appetizer, dip, make-ahead, slow]
ingredients:
  - 3 cups | plain whole-milk yogurt
  - 1 tsp | kosher salt
  - 3 tbsp | olive oil, plus more to finish
  - 1 tbsp | za'atar
  - To finish | flaky salt
method:
  - Stir the salt into the yogurt until it's fully dissolved.
  - Line a colander with a few layers of cheesecloth and set it over a bowl deep enough to keep the base clear of the liquid that collects.
  - Scrape the yogurt into the lined colander, gather the edges of the cloth over the top, and refrigerate.
  - Let drain, checking after 24 hours, until the yogurt has shrunk by about half and holds a clean edge when you drag a spoon through it — up to 48 hours for a firmer, cheese-like spread.
  - Unwrap and discard the whey that's collected in the bowl, then scrape the labneh into a shallow serving dish.
  - Swirl the surface with the back of a spoon, dimpling it, and pour the olive oil into the dimples.
  - Scatter the za'atar and flaky salt over the top.
---

The salt is doing the work, not the fridge — it draws water out of the yogurt by osmosis, and the cheesecloth just gives that water somewhere to go. Use whole-milk yogurt with nothing added to stabilize it; the low-fat and gelatin-thickened kinds hold onto their whey and never drain past a loose paste.

Twenty-four hours gets a soft, spreadable curd; go the full 48 if you want something firm enough to roll into balls and store under oil. Either way it keeps a week in the fridge, and the drained whey isn't waste — it's good in a smoothie or in place of buttermilk in the pancake batter.
