---
title: Cacio e Pepe
date: 2026-08-05 08:00:03 -0400
description: Just pasta, black pepper, and two grated cheeses, emulsified with starchy water into a sauce that clings without a drop of cream.
image: /assets/images/Cacio-E-Pepe.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 5 min
cook_time: 15 min
tags: [pasta, vegetarian, quick, main, cheese]
ingredients:
  - 1 lb | spaghetti or tonnarelli
  - 2 tbsp | coarse black pepper
  - 2 cups | finely grated Pecorino Romano
  - 1 cup | finely grated Parmesan
  - To taste | salt
method:
  - Toast the black pepper in a dry skillet over medium heat until fragrant, about 1 minute, then remove from the heat.
  - Bring a large pot of lightly salted water to a boil and cook the pasta until just shy of al dente.
  - Ladle out 2 cups of the starchy pasta water before draining, then drain the pasta.
  - Whisk the Pecorino and Parmesan with a splash of the hot pasta water in a wide bowl until it forms a loose, clump-free paste.
  - Return the skillet with the pepper to low heat, add a ladle of pasta water, and add the drained pasta.
  - Toss in the cheese paste a spoonful at a time, adding more pasta water as needed, until the sauce turns glossy and coats every strand.
  - Serve immediately, since the sauce tightens fast as it cools.
---

The whole dish lives or dies on temperature: cheese added off heat or into water that's too hot seizes into clumps instead of melting into a sauce, so keep the skillet on low and add the Pecorino gradually. Whisking it first into a paste with a little pasta water is the insurance policy — it's much harder to break a paste than to melt cheese straight into a hot pan.

No Pecorino Romano on hand is not a good excuse to skip this — a sharp aged Parmesan alone will work, though the sauce loses some of its tang.
