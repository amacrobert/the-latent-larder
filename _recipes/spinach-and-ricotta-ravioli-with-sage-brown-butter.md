---
title: Spinach and Ricotta Ravioli with Sage Brown Butter
date: 2026-07-30 03:00:03 -0400
description: Hand-filled pasta squares of ricotta and spinach, finished in butter browned until it smells like hazelnuts.
image: /assets/images/Spinach-And-Ricotta-Ravioli-With-Sage-Brown-Butter.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 1 hr 30 min
cook_time: 10 min
tags: [pasta, vegetarian, main, slow]
ingredients:
  - 2 cups | 00 flour, plus more for dusting
  - 3 | eggs
  - A pinch | salt
  - 2 cups | baby spinach
  - 1 cup | ricotta, drained
  - ½ cup | grated parmesan, plus more to serve
  - 1 | egg yolk
  - A pinch | nutmeg
  - 6 tbsp | butter
  - A handful | fresh sage leaves
  - To finish | lemon zest
method:
  - Mound the flour on a board, make a well, and crack in the eggs and salt.
  - Work the eggs into the flour with a fork until shaggy, then knead by hand until the dough is smooth and springs back when poked, about 8 minutes.
  - Wrap the dough and let it rest at room temperature until relaxed, about 30 minutes.
  - Wilt the spinach in a dry skillet until collapsed, then squeeze it dry and chop fine.
  - Stir the spinach with the ricotta, parmesan, egg yolk, and nutmeg into a thick, cohesive filling.
  - Roll the dough through a pasta machine, working down to the thinnest setting, until you can nearly read through the sheet.
  - Spoon small mounds of filling across one sheet, brush water around each mound, lay a second sheet on top, and press out the air before sealing.
  - Cut into squares with a knife or fluted cutter, pressing the edges firmly closed so nothing leaks.
  - Boil the ravioli in salted water until they float and the edges taste tender, 3 to 4 minutes.
  - Melt the butter with the sage in a skillet, swirling, until the butter turns golden brown and smells nutty.
  - Toss the drained ravioli in the brown butter and serve with lemon zest and extra parmesan.
---

The dough needs the full rest — skip it and the gluten fights you every time you roll, tearing instead of stretching thin. Roll it thinner than feels reasonable; a sheet that still looks opaque will taste doughy once cooked, even with the filling.

Seal the edges harder than seems necessary, too. A ravioli that opens in the pot dumps its filling into the water and boils up an empty square. Extra ravioli freeze well uncooked on a floured tray — once solid, bag them and boil straight from frozen, adding a minute to the cook time.
