---
title: Basil Pesto Pasta with Toasted Pine Nuts
date: 2026-08-01 14:00:02 -0400
description: A raw, blitzed basil sauce clinging to hot pasta — ready faster than the water takes to boil.
image: /assets/images/Basil-Pesto-Pasta.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 10 min
tags: [pasta, vegetarian, quick]
ingredients:
  - 1 lb | pasta (fusilli or spaghetti)
  - 3 cups | fresh basil leaves, packed
  - ¼ cup | pine nuts, toasted
  - 2 cloves | garlic, roughly chopped
  - ¾ cup | olive oil
  - ¾ cup | Parmesan, grated, plus more to finish
  - To taste | salt and black pepper
method:
  - Bring a large pot of well-salted water to a boil and cook the pasta until just shy of al dente.
  - While the water heats, toast the pine nuts in a dry skillet over medium heat, shaking often, until golden and fragrant, 3 to 4 minutes.
  - In a food processor, pulse the basil, garlic, and toasted pine nuts until finely chopped.
  - With the machine running, stream in the olive oil until the mixture is a loose, bright green paste.
  - Scrape into a bowl, stir in the Parmesan by hand, and season with salt and pepper.
  - Before draining the pasta, scoop out ½ cup of the starchy cooking water.
  - Toss the hot, drained pasta with the pesto, loosening with splashes of the reserved pasta water until it coats every strand glossy, not gluey.
  - Finish with more Parmesan and a crack of black pepper.
---

Blitz the pesto raw and never let it near direct heat — the food processor blade generates enough friction warmth on its own, and any more will dull the basil from bright green to a tired olive color and flatten the flavor. Toasting the pine nuts first is what makes a five-minute sauce taste like more than the sum of its parts.

No food processor, or want it more rustic: a mortar and pestle works, pound the garlic and basil to a paste before adding the nuts and cheese. Walnuts are a fine, cheaper stand-in for pine nuts — pound for pound they're just as good toasted.
