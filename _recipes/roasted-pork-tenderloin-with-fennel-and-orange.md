---
title: Roasted Pork Tenderloin with Fennel and Orange
date: 2026-07-31 01:00:03 -0400
description: A lean roast that's done before it dries out — sliced fennel and orange caramelize underneath it in the same skillet.
image: /assets/images/Roasted-Pork-Tenderloin-With-Fennel-And-Orange.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 25 min
tags: [pork, roasted, main, quick]
ingredients:
  - 1½ lb | pork tenderloin
  - 1 | fennel bulb, sliced
  - 1 | orange, sliced thin, plus zest
  - 2 tbsp | olive oil
  - 2 cloves | garlic, minced
  - 1 tsp | fennel seeds, cracked
  - A handful | fresh dill, to finish
  - To taste | salt and pepper
method:
  - Pat the tenderloin dry and season all over with salt, pepper, and the cracked fennel seeds.
  - Toss the sliced fennel and orange with the olive oil and garlic in a large oven-safe skillet.
  - Push the fennel to the edges and sear the tenderloin in the same skillet over high heat until browned on all sides, about 2 minutes per side.
  - Transfer the skillet to a 425°F oven and roast until an instant-read thermometer reads 140°F at the thickest point, 15 to 20 minutes.
  - Rest the pork on a cutting board for 5 minutes — it will climb to 145°F as it sits — then slice.
  - Scatter the sliced pork and roasted fennel with orange zest and dill before serving.
---

Tenderloin is the one cut of pork that punishes hesitation — it's lean enough to go from rosy to chalky in the time it takes to answer the phone, so the thermometer isn't optional here. Pulling it at 140°F and letting carryover heat finish the job is what keeps it juicy.

The fennel and orange aren't just a bed for the meat — they render into a sweet, slightly bitter pan sauce as the pork rests, so scrape the skillet when you plate. Pears work in place of the orange if that's what's on hand; skip the zest in that case.
