---
title: Buttermilk Skillet Cornbread with Honey Butter
date: 2026-07-30 22:30:03 -0400
description: A savory quick bread baked in a screaming-hot cast iron skillet for a crust the batter can't get any other way.
image: /assets/images/Buttermilk-Skillet-Cornbread-With-Honey-Butter.webp
servings: 8
prep_time: 10 min
cook_time: 25 min
tags: [bread, baking, side, vegetarian, quick]
ingredients:
  - 1½ cups | cornmeal
  - 1 cup | all-purpose flour
  - ¼ cup | sugar
  - 1 tbsp | baking powder
  - ½ tsp | baking soda
  - ¾ tsp | salt
  - 1½ cups | buttermilk
  - 2 | eggs
  - 4 tbsp | butter, melted, plus more for the pan
  - ¼ cup | butter, softened
  - 2 tbsp | honey
  - To finish | flaky salt
method:
  - Set a 10-inch cast iron skillet in the oven and heat the oven to 450°F.
  - Whisk the cornmeal, flour, sugar, baking powder, baking soda, and salt in a large bowl.
  - In a separate bowl, whisk the buttermilk, eggs, and melted butter until smooth.
  - Pour the wet ingredients into the dry and stir just until no dry streaks remain.
  - Pull the hot skillet from the oven, add a pat of butter, and swirl until it foams and coats the pan.
  - Pour in the batter — it should sizzle loudly at the edges — and return the skillet to the oven.
  - Bake until the top is deep golden brown and a skewer comes out clean, about 20 minutes.
  - Let the cornbread rest in the skillet for 10 minutes before cutting into wedges.
  - Mash the softened butter with the honey and a pinch of flaky salt, and serve alongside.
---

The skillet is doing the real work here: heated empty in the oven until it's almost smoking, it sears the bottom the instant the batter hits it, so you get a shattering, deep-brown crust instead of the pale underside a cold pan gives you. Buttermilk keeps the crumb tender and adds enough tang to balance the sugar — don't swap in sweet milk, or it reads more like cake.

This is built to sit next to something with a lot of sauce or braising liquid — chili, gumbo, a pot of beans — that a wedge can go mop up. The honey butter is optional but it's a five-second addition and nobody has ever complained about it being on the table.
