---
title: Roasted Butternut Squash Soup with Crispy Sage
date: 2026-07-26
description: A blender soup that tastes like more work than it is, finished with butter browned until it smells like a bakery.
image: /assets/images/Roasted-Butternut-Squash-Soup-With-Crispy-Sage.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 1 hr
tags: [soup, vegetarian, roasted, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 3 lb | butternut squash, halved and seeded
  - 3 tbsp | olive oil
  - 1 | yellow onion, chopped
  - 3 cloves | garlic
  - 4 cups | vegetable stock
  - ¼ cup | heavy cream
  - 4 tbsp | unsalted butter
  - 12 | fresh sage leaves
  - Salt and pepper, to taste
method:
  - Heat the oven to 425°F. Rub the cut sides of the squash with 1 tablespoon of the olive oil and set cut-side down on a sheet pan.
  - Roast until a knife slides through the thickest part with no resistance, 40 to 45 minutes.
  - Meanwhile, warm the remaining olive oil in a soup pot over medium heat and cook the onion, stirring occasionally, until soft and translucent, about 8 minutes.
  - Add the garlic and cook until fragrant, about 1 minute.
  - Scoop the roasted squash flesh into the pot, discarding the skins, and pour in the stock.
  - Bring to a simmer and cook for 15 minutes, then puree with an immersion blender until completely smooth.
  - Stir in the cream and season with salt and pepper.
  - Melt the butter in a small skillet over medium heat and add the sage leaves; cook until the butter smells nutty and turns the color of light caramel and the leaves have gone crisp and dark, about 3 minutes.
  - Ladle the soup into bowls and top each with crispy sage leaves and a spoonful of the browned butter.
---

Roast the squash instead of boiling it — boiling waterlogs it and you spend the rest of the recipe trying to cook that water back out. The skins should peel away from the flesh with a spoon once roasted; if they're still clinging, it needs a few more minutes in the oven.

The brown butter isn't a garnish, it's the second half of the flavor — don't skip it for a drizzle of plain olive oil. No sage on hand, a spoonful of chili crisp does a different but equally good job of cutting the sweetness.
