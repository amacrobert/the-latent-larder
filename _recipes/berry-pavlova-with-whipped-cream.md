---
title: Berry Pavlova with Whipped Cream
date: 2026-07-30 11:30:03 -0400
description: A crisp meringue shell hiding a marshmallow center, piled with cream and whatever berries are ripest.
image: /assets/images/Berry-Pavlova-With-Whipped-Cream.webp
servings: 6
prep_time: 20 min
cook_time: 1 hr 30 min
tags: [dessert, baking, fruit, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 6 | egg whites, room temperature
  - 1½ cups | granulated sugar
  - 1½ tsp | cornstarch
  - 1½ tsp | white vinegar
  - 1½ tsp | vanilla extract
  - 1 cup | heavy cream, cold
  - 2 tbsp | powdered sugar
  - 2 cups | mixed berries
  - To finish | mint leaves
method:
  - Preheat the oven to 250°F and trace a 9-inch circle on a sheet of parchment, then flip it ink-side down on a baking sheet.
  - Beat the egg whites on medium speed until foamy, then raise to high and beat until they hold a soft peak.
  - Add the sugar a tablespoon at a time, beating well between additions, until the meringue is thick, glossy, and holds a stiff peak when the beater is lifted.
  - Sift the cornstarch over the meringue, add the vinegar and vanilla, and fold in just until combined.
  - Mound the meringue inside the traced circle, building the edge slightly higher than the center to cradle the cream later.
  - Bake until the outside is dry, pale, and lifts cleanly off the parchment, about 1 hour 15 minutes.
  - Turn off the oven and leave the pavlova inside with the door shut for at least 1 hour, until fully cool.
  - Whip the cream with the powdered sugar to soft peaks just before serving.
  - Spoon the cream over the cooled meringue and scatter the berries and mint on top.
---

The center should still give like a marshmallow when you cut into it — that's the cornstarch and vinegar doing their job, not an underbaked meringue. If it comes out chewy all the way through instead of shattering at the edge, the oven ran a touch hot.

Bake and cool the shell a day ahead and keep it somewhere dry; humidity is what turns a crisp pavlova sticky overnight. Don't add the cream and fruit until the last possible minute, since the moment they touch the meringue it starts to soften.
