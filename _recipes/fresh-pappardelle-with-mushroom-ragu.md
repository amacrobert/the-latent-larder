---
title: Fresh Pappardelle with Mushroom Ragù
date: 2026-07-29
description: Egg dough rolled thin by hand and cut into ribbons, tossed with a mushroom ragù built to taste like it simmered all day.
image: /assets/images/Fresh-Pappardelle-With-Mushroom-Ragu.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 45 min
cook_time: 30 min
tags: [pasta, vegetarian, main, slow]
ingredients:
  - 2 cups | 00 or all-purpose flour
  - 3 | eggs
  - 1 tbsp | olive oil
  - ½ tsp | salt
  - 1 oz | dried porcini mushrooms
  - 1 lb | cremini mushrooms, sliced
  - 1 | shallot, minced
  - 3 cloves | garlic, minced
  - 3 tbsp | butter
  - 2 tbsp | tomato paste
  - ½ cup | dry red wine
  - ½ cup | heavy cream
  - To finish | grated Parmesan and thyme
method:
  - Mound the flour on a board, make a well in the center, and add the eggs, olive oil, and salt; whisk with a fork until a shaggy dough forms.
  - Knead until the dough is smooth and springs back when pressed, about 8 minutes, then wrap and rest at room temperature for 30 minutes.
  - Cover the porcini in hot water and let them sit until soft, about 15 minutes; strain, reserving the liquid, and chop.
  - Melt the butter in a wide pan over medium heat and cook the shallot and garlic until soft, about 3 minutes.
  - Add the cremini mushrooms and cook, stirring occasionally, until they release their liquid and it boils away, then keep going until they brown.
  - Stir in the tomato paste and porcini, and cook until the paste darkens, about 1 minute.
  - Pour in the wine and simmer until the pan is nearly dry, then add the reserved mushroom liquid and cream and simmer until thick enough to coat the back of a spoon.
  - Roll the pasta dough out thin enough to see your hand through it, then cut into ¾-inch ribbons.
  - Boil the pappardelle in well-salted water until it floats to the surface, 2 to 3 minutes, then lift it straight into the ragù with a splash of the pasta water.
  - Toss until the sauce clings, then finish with Parmesan and thyme.
---

Rest the dough before rolling — skip it and the gluten fights back, snapping instead of stretching thin. The porcini soaking liquid is what gives the sauce its depth; strain it through a coffee filter if it's gritty, and don't pour off the last cloudy inch where the sand settles.

No pasta machine needed, just a rolling pin and patience, though a machine makes the ribbons more even. Dried pappardelle works in a pinch if you're short on time — the ragù is the part worth the effort, and it holds well in the fridge for a couple of days, better than the fresh dough does.
