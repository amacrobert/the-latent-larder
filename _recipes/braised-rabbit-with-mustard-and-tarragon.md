---
title: Braised Rabbit with Mustard and Tarragon
date: 2026-07-29 10:02:53 -0400
description: A whole rabbit browned and braised in wine and cream until the meat gives up the bone on its own, mustard and tarragon keeping it from tasting like plain chicken.
image: /assets/images/Braised-Rabbit-With-Mustard-And-Tarragon.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 20 min
cook_time: 1 hr 30 min
tags: [rabbit, slow, main, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 1 (3 lb) | rabbit, cut into 8 pieces
  - 2 tbsp | all-purpose flour
  - 3 tbsp | olive oil
  - 4 slices | bacon, chopped
  - 1 | onion, sliced
  - 2 | carrots, sliced
  - 2 cloves | garlic, minced
  - 1 cup | dry white wine
  - 1½ cups | chicken stock
  - 2 tbsp | Dijon mustard
  - ½ cup | heavy cream
  - A handful | tarragon, chopped
  - To taste | salt and pepper
method:
  - Season the rabbit pieces with salt and pepper, then dust them in the flour, shaking off the excess.
  - Heat the olive oil in a Dutch oven over medium-high and cook the bacon until crisp, then remove it with a slotted spoon.
  - Brown the rabbit in the bacon fat in batches, turning once the underside is deep gold, and set it aside with the bacon.
  - Add the onion and carrots to the pot and cook until the onion turns translucent.
  - Stir in the garlic and cook until fragrant, about 30 seconds.
  - Pour in the white wine and scrape up the browned bits stuck to the bottom of the pot.
  - Return the rabbit and bacon to the pot, add the stock, and bring to a simmer.
  - Cover and braise in a 325°F oven until the meat pulls easily from the bone, about 1 hour 15 minutes.
  - Transfer the rabbit to a platter, then whisk the mustard and cream into the braising liquid and simmer until it coats the back of a spoon.
  - Stir in the tarragon, spoon the sauce over the rabbit, and serve.
---

Rabbit is lean enough that a quick cook turns it stringy, which is exactly why it wants this treatment instead — an hour and change at a low simmer melts the connective tissue without drying out the meat the way it would a chicken thigh. Buy it whole and have the butcher break it down, or ask for it already cut into pieces.

If rabbit isn't easy to find, bone-in chicken thighs work in the same braise, though they'll need closer to 45 minutes rather than the full hour and change. Serve it over egg noodles or mashed potato — something that can sit under the sauce and soak it up.
