---
title: Crispy Skillet Hash Browns
date: 2026-08-09 03:00:03 -0400
description: Shredded potatoes pressed into a hot buttered skillet until the bottom turns into one solid, shattering crust — the only trick is getting the water out first.
image: /assets/images/Crispy-Skillet-Hash-Browns.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 20 min
tags: [breakfast, side, quick, vegetarian, gluten-free]
ingredients:
  - 2 lb | russet potatoes, peeled
  - ½ | yellow onion, finely diced
  - 3 tbsp | unsalted butter
  - 2 tbsp | vegetable oil
  - To taste | salt and pepper
method:
  - Grate the potatoes on the large holes of a box grater into a bowl of cold water, swishing them around until the water turns cloudy, then drain.
  - Spread the shredded potato on a clean kitchen towel, gather the corners, and wring hard over the sink until no more water drips out.
  - Toss the wrung-out potato with the onion, 1 teaspoon salt, and a few grinds of pepper.
  - Melt the butter with the oil in a large nonstick or cast-iron skillet over medium-high heat until it shimmers.
  - Press the potato mixture into an even layer across the pan, packing it down firmly with a spatula.
  - Cook undisturbed until the bottom is deep golden brown and holds together as a crust, 8 to 10 minutes.
  - Cut the cake into quarters, flip each piece, and press down again.
  - Cook the second side until equally browned and crisp at the edges, another 6 to 8 minutes.
  - Slide onto a plate and season with more salt while hot.
---

Wringing the potatoes dry is the whole recipe — any water left behind steams instead of frying, and steamed shreds turn gray and gummy instead of crisping. Squeeze harder than seems necessary; a cup of shredded potato should give up several tablespoons of starchy water.

Cutting the cake into quarters before flipping beats trying to flip it whole — a home skillet rarely has the momentum for a clean toss, and a broken hash brown still tastes fine but looks like a mistake. Good under a fried egg, or with hot sauce and nothing else.
