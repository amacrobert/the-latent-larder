---
title: Double-Fried Gochujang Chicken Wings
date: 2026-08-10 21:00:02 -0400
description: Fried twice for a crust that stays shattering-crisp under a sticky, spicy-sweet gochujang glaze.
image: /assets/images/Gochujang-Chicken-Wings.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 30 min
tags: [chicken, appetizer, spicy]
ingredients:
  - 3 lb | chicken wings, split at the joint
  - 1 tsp | kosher salt
  - ½ tsp | black pepper
  - ½ cup | potato starch
  - For frying | vegetable oil
  - 4 cloves | garlic, minced
  - 1 tsp | grated fresh ginger
  - 1 tbsp | unsalted butter
  - ¼ cup | gochujang
  - 2 tbsp | honey
  - 2 tbsp | soy sauce
  - 1 tbsp | rice vinegar
  - To finish | sesame seeds and sliced scallions
method:
  - Pat the wings completely dry, then toss with the salt and pepper.
  - Toss the wings in the potato starch until every piece is evenly coated, shaking off the excess.
  - Heat 3 inches of oil in a Dutch oven to 325°F and fry the wings in batches until pale gold and just cooked through, about 6 minutes, then drain on a wire rack.
  - Bring the oil up to 375°F and fry the wings a second time in batches until deep mahogany brown and stiff to the touch, about 4 minutes, then drain again.
  - While the wings rest, melt the butter in a saucepan over medium heat, add the garlic and ginger, and cook until fragrant, about 30 seconds.
  - Whisk in the gochujang, honey, soy sauce, and rice vinegar, and simmer until glossy and thick enough to coat the back of a spoon, about 2 minutes.
  - Toss the hot wings in the glaze until fully coated, scatter with sesame seeds and scallions, and serve immediately.
---

The first fry cooks the wing through and sets the starch crust at a temperature low enough not to burn it; the second, hotter fry is what actually makes it shatter, and is the only reason the crust survives getting tossed in a wet glaze without going soft. Don't skip the rest between fries — the crust needs a minute to firm up before it goes back in.

No gochujang on hand, swap in three parts sriracha to one part brown sugar — it won't have the same fermented depth, but it'll still glaze.
