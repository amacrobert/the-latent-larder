---
title: Crispy Chicken and Black Bean Burritos
date: 2026-08-01 03:30:02 -0400
description: Chili-rubbed chicken thighs, lime rice, and black beans rolled tight and browned seam-down until the tortilla holds its own shape.
servings: 4
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 20 min
tags: [chicken, beans, quick, main, spicy, cheese]
ingredients:
  - 1 lb | chicken thighs, boneless skinless
  - 1 tbsp | chili powder
  - 1 tsp | ground cumin
  - 1 tsp | smoked paprika
  - To taste | salt and pepper
  - 2 tbsp | olive oil
  - 15 oz | canned black beans, drained and rinsed
  - 2 cups | cooked white rice
  - 2 tbsp | lime juice
  - A handful | cilantro, chopped
  - 4 | large flour tortillas (10-inch)
  - 1 cup | shredded Monterey Jack
  - ½ cup | salsa
  - ¼ cup | sour cream
method:
  - Season the chicken thighs all over with chili powder, cumin, smoked paprika, salt, and pepper.
  - Heat 1 tablespoon of the oil in a skillet over medium-high and cook the chicken 5-6 minutes per side, until the juices run clear and the outside is deeply browned.
  - Rest the chicken 5 minutes, then chop into bite-sized pieces.
  - Warm the black beans in a small saucepan with a splash of water, mashing a few against the side of the pot, until heated through.
  - Toss the rice with the lime juice and cilantro.
  - Warm the tortillas until pliable, then layer rice, beans, chicken, cheese, salsa, and sour cream across the center of each.
  - Fold in the sides and roll tightly into a burrito, seam-side down.
  - Heat the remaining oil in the wiped-out skillet over medium and set each burrito seam-side down for 1-2 minutes per side, until the tortilla crisps and holds its seal.
---

The seam-down sear is what keeps these from falling apart on the second bite — a burrito rolled tight and never toasted just unrolls itself in your hands. Give the tortilla those two minutes even if everything inside is already warm; it's sealing the wrap, not reheating the filling.

Shredded rotisserie chicken works in place of the seared thighs if you're short on time — skip straight to warming it through with the spices in the skillet. Pinto beans are a fine swap for black, and a scoop of guacamole is worth adding if you have it.
