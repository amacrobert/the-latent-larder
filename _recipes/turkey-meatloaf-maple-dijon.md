---
title: Turkey Meatloaf with Maple-Dijon Glaze
date: 2026-08-08 03:00:03 -0400
description: Ground turkey loosened with grated zucchini and packed into a loaf pan for structure, glazed with maple syrup and Dijon instead of ketchup.
image: /assets/images/Turkey-Meatloaf-Maple-Dijon.webp
servings: 6
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 55 min
tags: [turkey, baking, main, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 2 lb | ground turkey (93/7)
  - 1 | medium zucchini, grated and squeezed dry
  - ¾ cup | dry breadcrumbs
  - ¼ cup | whole milk
  - 1 | small yellow onion, finely diced
  - 2 cloves | garlic, minced
  - 2 | large eggs
  - 1 tbsp | Dijon mustard
  - 1 tbsp | Worcestershire sauce
  - 1 tsp | kosher salt
  - ½ tsp | black pepper
  - ¼ cup | maple syrup
  - 3 tbsp | Dijon mustard
  - 1 tbsp | cider vinegar
method:
  - Preheat the oven to 350°F and grease a 9-by-5-inch loaf pan.
  - Squeeze the grated zucchini hard in a clean kitchen towel until no more water runs out.
  - Stir the breadcrumbs and milk together in a large bowl and let sit until the crumbs have soaked it up, about 5 minutes.
  - Add the turkey, zucchini, onion, garlic, eggs, 1 tbsp Dijon, Worcestershire, salt, and pepper and mix with your hands just until combined — overworking it makes the loaf dense.
  - Pack the mixture into the loaf pan and smooth the top.
  - Whisk the maple syrup, 3 tbsp Dijon, and cider vinegar together and brush half over the loaf.
  - Bake, brushing with the remaining glaze halfway through, until a thermometer inserted in the center reads 165°F, about 55 minutes.
  - Let the loaf rest for 10 minutes, then tip off any accumulated liquid before slicing.
---

Ground turkey has almost none of the fat that keeps a beef meatloaf moist on its own, so the zucchini is doing that job here — squeeze it dry or the loaf steams instead of browning. The loaf pan matters too: turkey is looser than a beef-and-pork mix, and without a pan wall to lean on it spreads flat on a sheet pan instead of holding a shape.

Skip the ketchup glaze this time; the maple and Dijon give it something sharper to push against the sweetness. Leftovers slice cleanly once fully cooled and reheat well in a skillet with a splash of water to loosen the glaze back up.
