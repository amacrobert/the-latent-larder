---
title: Saffron Rice with Crispy Tahdig
date: 2026-07-27
description: Basmati steamed until the bottom fuses into a shatter-crisp golden crust, then flipped onto a platter like a cake.
image: /assets/images/Saffron-Rice-With-Crispy-Tahdig.webp
servings: 6
prep_time: 20 min
cook_time: 1 hour
tags: [rice, side, vegetarian, slow]
ingredients:
  - 2 cups | basmati rice
  - ¼ cup | plain yogurt
  - ¼ tsp | saffron threads
  - 2 tbsp | hot water
  - ¼ cup | vegetable oil
  - 4 tbsp | unsalted butter, melted
  - 2 tbsp | salt, for the pot
method:
  - Rinse the rice in several changes of cold water until it runs clear, then soak in cold water for 30 minutes and drain.
  - Crumble the saffron into the hot water and let it bloom until deep orange, about 5 minutes.
  - Bring a large pot of water with the 2 tablespoons of salt to a boil, add the rice, and parboil for 6 to 7 minutes, until the grains are tender at the edge but still firm at the core.
  - Drain the rice and toss 2 cups of it in a bowl with the yogurt and half the saffron water until evenly coated.
  - Heat the oil and butter in a nonstick pot over medium heat until shimmering.
  - Spread the saffron-yogurt rice over the bottom of the pot in an even layer, pressing it flat.
  - Mound the remaining plain rice on top into a pyramid, then poke a few holes down to the bottom with a spoon handle to let steam through.
  - Drizzle the rest of the saffron water over the top, wrap the lid in a clean kitchen towel, and cover the pot tightly.
  - Steam over low heat for 45 minutes, until you can hear the bottom crackling and smell it turning toasty.
  - Rest off the heat for 5 minutes, then run a spatula around the edge and invert the pot onto a platter in one motion so the crust comes out intact.
---

The yogurt-saffron layer isn't just for color — it's what keeps the bottom rice from drying out before it has time to crisp, and it's the reason the crust lifts out in one piece instead of shattering into flakes stuck to the pot. A nonstick pot matters more than usual here; the crust will fight you out of anything else.

Don't skip the towel under the lid. It catches the condensation that would otherwise drip back down and turn the crackling bottom soggy. Serve it crust-side up, broken apart at the table, alongside anything that wants a starch to sit under its sauce — a braise or a stew is the obvious partner.
