---
title: Oysters on the Half Shell with Mignonette
date: 2026-07-30 11:00:03 -0400
description: Raw oysters shucked to order and topped with a sharp shallot-vinegar mignonette, no stove involved at any point.
image: /assets/images/Oysters-On-The-Half-Shell-With-Mignonette.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 25 min
cook_time: 0 min
tags: [shellfish, appetizer, quick, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 2 dozen | oysters, scrubbed
  - ¼ cup | red wine vinegar
  - 2 tbsp | shallot, minced
  - ½ tsp | black pepper, cracked
  - To finish | lemon wedges
  - Crushed ice, for serving
method:
  - Stir the vinegar, shallot, and cracked pepper together and let it sit at least 15 minutes so the shallot mellows.
  - Holding each oyster flat-side up in a folded towel, work an oyster knife into the hinge and twist until it pops.
  - Slide the knife along the top shell to sever the muscle, then lift the shell away without spilling the liquor.
  - Run the knife under the oyster to free it from the bottom shell, keeping it sitting in its liquor.
  - Nestle the shucked oysters into a bed of crushed ice, spoon a little mignonette over each, and serve with lemon wedges.
---

The mignonette needs to steep before the oysters come out of the shell — do it too close to serving and the shallot still bites instead of rounding out. Buy from a fishmonger who can tell you the harvest date, and keep the oysters on ice right up until you shuck them.

Cupped, briny East Coast varieties hold their liquor better under a knife than flatter West Coast ones, which makes them more forgiving for a first attempt at shucking. Save the mignonette recipe — it's just as good over a dozen shucked and chilled ahead for a party, spooned on right before they hit the table.
