---
title: Chewy Snickerdoodle Cookies
date: 2026-08-04 15:00:03 -0400
description: Cream of tartar gives the tang and the crackled top — no chocolate, no mix-ins, just butter and cinnamon sugar doing the work.
image: /assets/images/Chewy-Snickerdoodle-Cookies.webp
servings: 24
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 11 min
tags: [dessert, baking, quick]
ingredients:
  - 2¾ cups | flour
  - 2 tsp | cream of tartar
  - 1 tsp | baking soda
  - ½ tsp | salt
  - 1 cup | unsalted butter, softened
  - 1½ cups | granulated sugar
  - 2 | eggs
  - 1 tsp | vanilla extract
  - ¼ cup | sugar, for coating
  - 1 tbsp | ground cinnamon, for coating
method:
  - Whisk together the flour, cream of tartar, baking soda, and salt in a bowl and set aside.
  - Beat the softened butter with the granulated sugar until pale and fluffy, about 3 minutes.
  - Beat in the eggs one at a time, then the vanilla, until fully combined.
  - Add the dry ingredients and mix on low just until no streaks of flour remain.
  - Stir together the coating sugar and cinnamon in a shallow bowl.
  - Roll the dough into 1½-tablespoon balls and toss each one in the cinnamon sugar until fully coated.
  - Arrange on parchment-lined sheet pans, spaced 2 inches apart.
  - Heat the oven to 375°F and bake until the edges have set and the tops have cracked but the centers still look pale and soft, 9 to 11 minutes.
  - Let cool on the pan for 5 minutes before moving to a rack.
---

Cream of tartar is the ingredient that makes a snickerdoodle a snickerdoodle rather than a plain sugar cookie — it's what gives the dough its faint tang and pushes the tops to crack instead of staying smooth. Don't swap it out; there's no substitute that does both jobs at once.

Pull the pan as soon as the tops have cracked, even if the centers look underdone — they firm up fast once they're off the heat, and a cookie that looks finished in the oven is already past chewy. Chill the dough for 20 minutes first if your kitchen runs warm; it keeps the balls from flattening before the crackle sets.
