title: Buttermilk Doughnuts with Vanilla Glaze
date: 2026-07-30 16:00:03 -0400
description: A yeasted dough proofed twice and dropped in hot oil for a minute a side — the only dessert in the larder that goes near a fryer.
image: /assets/images/Buttermilk-Doughnuts-With-Vanilla-Glaze.webp
servings: 12
prep_time: 1 hr 45 min
cook_time: 20 min
tags: [dessert, fried, baking, breakfast, vegetarian]
ingredients:
  - ¾ cup | warm buttermilk
  - 2¼ tsp | active dry yeast
  - ¼ cup | sugar, divided
  - 3½ cups | flour
  - 1 tsp | salt
  - 2 | eggs
  - ¼ cup | butter, melted
  - 1 tsp | vanilla extract
  - 4 cups | neutral oil, for frying
  - 1½ cups | powdered sugar
  - 3 tbsp | buttermilk
  - 1 tsp | vanilla extract
method:
  - Whisk the warm buttermilk, yeast, and a pinch of the sugar; let stand until foamy, about 5 minutes.
  - Whisk the flour, remaining sugar, and salt in a large bowl. Add the yeast mixture, eggs, melted butter, and vanilla, and mix until a shaggy dough comes together.
  - Knead until smooth and elastic, about 8 minutes.
  - Cover and let rise in a warm spot until doubled, about 1 hour.
  - Turn the dough onto a floured surface and roll to ½ inch thick. Cut rounds with a doughnut cutter, gathering and re-rolling the scraps.
  - Transfer the doughnuts and holes to floured parchment, cover loosely, and let rise until visibly puffy, about 30 minutes.
  - Heat the oil to 350°F in a deep, heavy pot. Fry the doughnuts in batches until deep golden, about 1 minute per side.
  - Drain on a wire rack set over paper towels.
  - Whisk the powdered sugar, buttermilk, and vanilla until smooth. Dip the warm doughnuts and let the glaze set before serving.
---

The oil temperature is the whole recipe. Below 350°F the dough sits and soaks instead of sealing, and you get a greasy doughnut with a pale ring around the middle; above 375°F the outside is mahogany before the center cooks through. A thermometer clipped to the pot earns its keep here — don't eyeball it.

Glaze while the doughnuts are still warm so it goes translucent and sets into a shell rather than sitting on top like frosting. If you'd rather skip the glaze, toss them straight from the rack in cinnamon sugar instead.
