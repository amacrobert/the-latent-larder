---
title: Garlic-Ginger Cashew Chicken Stir-Fry
date: 2026-08-05 20:00:03 -0400
description: Velveted chicken thighs and roasted cashews tossed in a glossy soy-garlic sauce — faster than delivery and less sweet than the takeout version.
image: /assets/images/Garlic-Ginger-Cashew-Chicken-Stir-Fry.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 15 min
tags: [chicken, quick, main]
ingredients:
  - 1½ lbs | chicken thighs, cut into 1-inch pieces
  - 1 tbsp | soy sauce
  - 1 tbsp | cornstarch
  - 3 tbsp | soy sauce
  - 1 tbsp | oyster sauce
  - 1 tbsp | rice vinegar
  - 1 tbsp | brown sugar
  - ¼ cup | chicken stock
  - 1 tsp | cornstarch
  - 3 tbsp | vegetable oil, divided
  - 4 cloves | garlic, minced
  - 1 tbsp | ginger, minced
  - 1 | red bell pepper, cut into 1-inch pieces
  - 4 | scallions, cut into 1-inch pieces
  - ¾ cup | roasted cashews
  - To finish | steamed rice
method:
  - Toss the chicken with 1 tbsp soy sauce and 1 tbsp cornstarch. Let sit 10 minutes.
  - Whisk the remaining soy sauce, oyster sauce, rice vinegar, brown sugar, chicken stock, and 1 tsp cornstarch into a sauce and set aside.
  - Heat 2 tbsp oil in a large skillet or wok over high heat until it shimmers. Sear the chicken undisturbed until browned on one side, about 3 minutes, then stir-fry until just cooked through. Transfer to a plate.
  - Add the remaining 1 tbsp oil, the garlic, and the ginger. Stir until fragrant, about 30 seconds.
  - Add the bell pepper and stir-fry until it starts to soften at the edges, about 2 minutes.
  - Return the chicken to the pan, pour in the sauce, and toss until it thickens and coats everything, about 1 minute.
  - Stir in the cashews and scallions, then serve over rice.
---

Velveting the chicken in cornstarch is what keeps it silky through a hard sear instead of turning stringy — don't skip it, and don't crowd the pan or the chicken steams instead of browns. Toast raw cashews in a dry skillet for a few minutes first if the roasted, salted kind aren't on hand.

This is the everyday version, not the sweet, deep-fried takeout kind — the sauce leans savory and garlicky rather than sugary. Swap the bell pepper for snap peas or broccoli florets depending on what's in the fridge.
