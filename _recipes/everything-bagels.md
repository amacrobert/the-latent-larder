---
title: Everything Bagels
date: 2026-07-28 20:03:08 -0400
description: A stiff, low-hydration dough boiled before it's baked, the only way to get a shiny crust and a chew that fights back.
image: /assets/images/Everything-Bagels.webp
servings: 8
prep_time: 30 min (plus overnight rise)
cook_time: 25 min
tags: [bread, baking, breakfast, vegetarian, slow, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 4 cups | bread flour
  - 1½ tsp | instant yeast
  - 2 tbsp | sugar
  - 2 tsp | salt
  - 1¼ cups | warm water
  - 1 tbsp | honey, for the pot
  - 1 tsp | baking soda, for the pot
  - ¼ cup | everything bagel seasoning
  - To finish | cornmeal, for the sheet
method:
  - Whisk the yeast and sugar into the warm water and let stand until foamy on top, about 5 minutes.
  - Mix the flour and salt in a large bowl, pour in the yeast mixture, and knead by hand or mixer until the dough is stiff, smooth, and barely tacky, about 8 minutes — it should feel much tighter than a sandwich-bread dough.
  - Divide into 8 pieces and roll each into a tight ball, then poke a hole through the center with your thumb and stretch it into a ring about 2 inches wide.
  - Set the rings on a parchment-lined sheet, cover, and refrigerate overnight, 12 to 18 hours.
  - The next day, bring a wide pot of water to a boil and stir in the honey and baking soda.
  - Heat the oven to 425°F and dust a baking sheet with cornmeal.
  - Boil the bagels in batches, 1 minute per side, until they puff and turn glossy, then lift out with a slotted spoon back onto the sheet.
  - While still wet, press the tops into the everything seasoning and set seasoned-side up.
  - Bake until deep golden brown all over, 20 to 25 minutes, rotating the sheet halfway through.
  - Cool on a rack at least 15 minutes before slicing.
---

The boil is what separates a bagel from a very dense dinner roll — it gelatinizes the surface starch before the oven ever gets to it, which is where the shine and the chew both come from. Skip it and you've made a bread ring, not a bagel. Keep the dough on the stiff side of what feels right; a wetter dough boils up soft and loses the shape you spent time rolling.

The overnight cold rise isn't optional either — it's what gives a plain flour-water-yeast dough enough flavor to stand up to nothing but a schmear. They keep at room temperature a day, sliced and frozen for a month after that, and take well to whatever seed mix you've got instead of the everything blend.
