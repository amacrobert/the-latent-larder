---
title: Crispy Vegetable Samosas with Tamarind Chutney
date: 2026-08-04 07:00:03 -0400
description: Hand-folded pastry cones packed with spiced potato and pea, fried low and slow until the shell shatters without a raw streak inside.
image: /assets/images/Vegetable-Samosas.webp
servings: 6
prep_time: 45 min
cook_time: 30 min
tags: [vegetarian, appetizer, fried, spicy, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 2 cups | all-purpose flour
  - 6 tbsp | vegetable oil, plus more for frying
  - 1 tsp | ajwain (carom seeds), crushed
  - 1 tsp | salt
  - About ½ cup | cold water
  - 1½ lb | russet potatoes, peeled and cubed
  - 1 | small onion, finely chopped
  - 1 tbsp | ginger, grated
  - 2 | green chiles, minced
  - 1 tsp | cumin seeds
  - 1 cup | frozen peas
  - 1 tsp | garam masala
  - 1 tsp | ground coriander
  - ½ tsp | turmeric
  - 1 tbsp | lemon juice
  - ¼ cup | cilantro, chopped
  - To finish | tamarind chutney
method:
  - Combine the flour, ajwain, and salt, then rub in the oil with your fingers until the mixture holds together when pinched.
  - Add the cold water a little at a time, kneading to a stiff, smooth dough. Cover and rest 30 minutes.
  - Boil the potatoes until fork-tender, drain, and roughly mash, leaving some chunks.
  - Heat 3 tablespoons oil and toast the cumin seeds until they pop, then add the onion, ginger, and chiles and cook until the onion is soft and golden.
  - Stir in the peas, garam masala, coriander, and turmeric and cook until the peas are heated through and the spices smell toasted.
  - Fold in the mashed potatoes, lemon juice, cilantro, and salt to taste. Cook, stirring, until the filling holds together, then spread it out to cool completely.
  - Divide the dough into 8 balls. Roll each into a thin oval, cut in half, and form each half into a cone, sealing the straight edge with a dab of water.
  - Fill each cone with a heaping tablespoon of cooled filling, then pleat and seal the top edge shut.
  - Heat 2 inches of oil to 325°F. Fry the samosas in batches, turning occasionally, for 12 to 15 minutes, until deep golden and blistered.
  - Drain on a rack and serve hot with tamarind chutney.
---

Fry these slower and cooler than instinct says to — 325°F, not the usual 350–375°F. A hotter oil browns the shell before the raw dough at the center has cooked through, which is the single way this goes wrong. The low, patient fry is what gets you a shell that shatters clean instead of one with a doughy interior.

Let the filling go completely cold before you fill the cones. Warm filling sweats, and that moisture is enough to soften the dough and blow out a seam in the oil. No deep fryer needed — a heavy pot with a thermometer clipped to the side works fine, and a spider or slotted spoon does the rest.
