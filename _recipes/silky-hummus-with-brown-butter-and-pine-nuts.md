---
title: Silky Hummus with Brown Butter and Pine Nuts
date: 2026-08-01 07:00:02 -0400
description: Canned chickpeas simmered until their skins slip off, blitzed past the point most home hummus stops, then pooled with butter browned until it smells nutty.
image: /assets/images/Silky-Hummus-With-Brown-Butter-And-Pine-Nuts.webp
servings: 6
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 15 min
tags: [appetizer, dip, vegetarian, make-ahead, quick]
ingredients:
  - 2 cans | chickpeas (15 oz each), rinsed
  - 1 tsp | baking soda
  - ¼ cup | tahini
  - ¼ cup | lemon juice
  - 1 clove | garlic
  - ¼ cup | ice water
  - ½ tsp | salt
  - 4 tbsp | butter
  - ¼ cup | pine nuts
  - To finish | paprika, parsley
method:
  - Simmer the chickpeas with the baking soda in 3 cups water for 10 minutes, until the skins loosen and float free.
  - Drain the chickpeas and rub them between your hands under cool running water until most of the skins wash away.
  - Blend the tahini and lemon juice in a food processor for a full minute, until the mixture turns pale and creamy.
  - Add the garlic, salt, and chickpeas, and process until mostly smooth.
  - With the processor running, stream in the ice water and blend for 2 minutes, until the hummus turns fluffy and lightens in color.
  - Melt the butter in a small skillet over medium heat, swirling constantly, until it turns amber and smells nutty.
  - Add the pine nuts to the browned butter and toast for 1 minute, until golden.
  - Spread the hummus in a shallow bowl, swirl a well in the center, and pour the brown butter and pine nuts into it. Finish with paprika and parsley.
---

The baking soda simmer is what separates this from the dense, gluey hummus
that comes from just draining a can and hitting it with a blender. Softening
and sloughing off the skins is what lets the chickpeas actually break down
into something smooth instead of grainy — skip it and no amount of processing
time will fix the texture. The long blend with ice water afterward is doing
real work too: that's what whips air into the paste and turns it pale.

The brown butter is a departure from the usual olive-oil pool on top, but it
holds up the same way — pour it on right before serving so it stays warm
against the cold hummus. Warm pita or torn flatbread is the only accompaniment
this needs.
