---
title: Loaded Baked Potato Soup with Bacon and Cheddar
date: 2026-08-04 17:00:03 -0400
description: Potatoes simmered until they half-collapse into a roux-thickened broth, then loaded with everything that makes a baked potato worth eating.
image: /assets/images/Loaded-Baked-Potato-Soup.webp
servings: 6
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 35 min
tags: [soup, potato, pork, quick, cheese, main]
ingredients:
  - 2 lb | russet potatoes, peeled, ¾-inch cubes
  - 6 slices | bacon, chopped
  - 4 tbsp | butter
  - 1 | yellow onion, diced
  - 3 cloves | garlic, minced
  - ¼ cup | flour
  - 4 cups | chicken broth
  - 1 cup | heavy cream
  - 1½ cups | sharp cheddar, shredded, plus more to serve
  - ½ cup | sour cream
  - 4 | scallions, thinly sliced
  - To taste | salt and pepper
method:
  - Cook the bacon in a Dutch oven over medium heat, stirring occasionally, until crisp, then transfer to a paper-towel-lined plate and leave the fat in the pot.
  - Add the onion to the bacon fat and cook, stirring occasionally, until soft and translucent, about 6 minutes.
  - Add the butter and garlic and cook until fragrant, about 1 minute.
  - Sprinkle in the flour and stir constantly until it smells toasted and coats the bottom of the pot, about 2 minutes.
  - Whisk in the chicken broth a little at a time, scraping the bottom of the pot, until smooth.
  - Add the potatoes, bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer and cook until they're fork-tender, 15 to 18 minutes.
  - Mash about a third of the potatoes against the side of the pot with a spoon to thicken the broth while leaving the rest in chunks.
  - Stir in the cream and cheddar by the handful, letting each addition melt before adding the next, then stir in half the bacon.
  - Season with salt and pepper and ladle into bowls, topping each with the remaining bacon, a spoonful of sour cream, and the scallions.
---

The partial mash is what separates this from a purée — smash a third of the potatoes against the pot to thicken the broth, and leave the rest whole so every spoonful still has something to chew on. A blender makes it smooth and forgettable; a spoon against the side of the pot makes it a baked potato.

Boiling the potatoes is the weeknight version. For a deeper, less starchy-water flavor, bake them whole the day before, scoop the flesh into the broth in step 6, and skip the simmer — you're just heating it through and mashing to texture.
