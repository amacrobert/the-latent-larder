---
title: Buttermilk Biscuits and Sausage Gravy
date: 2026-08-09 06:00:02 -0400
description: Warm split biscuits under a peppery pan gravy built from the sausage fat left behind — the diner breakfast that needs a fork.
image: /assets/images/Biscuits-And-Sausage-Gravy.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 25 min
tags: [breakfast, pork, baking, main]
ingredients:
  - 2 cups | all-purpose flour
  - 1 tbsp | baking powder
  - 1 tsp | salt
  - 6 tbsp | cold unsalted butter, cubed
  - 1 cup | cold buttermilk
  - 1 lb | breakfast sausage, casings removed
  - ¼ cup | all-purpose flour
  - 3 cups | whole milk
  - 1 tsp | black pepper, plus more to taste
  - To taste | salt
method:
  - Whisk the 2 cups flour, baking powder, and 1 teaspoon salt together in a large bowl.
  - Cut the cold butter into the flour with your fingers until the largest pieces are pea-sized and the mixture looks sandy.
  - Pour in the buttermilk and fold just until a shaggy dough holds together.
  - Turn the dough onto a floured counter, pat it into a 1-inch-thick round, and cut into 8 biscuits, setting them on a parchment-lined sheet with their sides touching.
  - Bake at 425°F until the tops are deep gold and the sides have risen tall, 15 to 18 minutes.
  - While the biscuits bake, crumble the sausage into a cold skillet and cook over medium heat, breaking it up, until browned and no pink remains, about 8 minutes.
  - Sprinkle the ¼ cup flour over the sausage and stir constantly for 2 minutes, until it coats everything like wet sand and smells toasted.
  - Pour in the milk in a slow stream, whisking to work out any lumps, and bring to a simmer.
  - Cook, stirring often, until the gravy coats the back of a spoon and holds a trail when you drag a finger through it, 5 to 7 minutes.
  - Stir in the black pepper and season with salt to taste.
  - Split the biscuits and ladle the gravy over the warm halves.
---

The gravy's whole texture depends on that 2 minutes of stirring flour into the sausage fat before any liquid goes in — skip it and the gravy tastes like wet flour no matter how long it simmers afterward. Add the milk slowly and keep whisking; dumping it in all at once is the fastest way to a lumpy pot.

Regular pork breakfast sausage works best for the fat it renders, but hot Italian sausage makes a sharper, spicier version if that's what's on hand. Leftover gravy keeps in the fridge for a couple of days and reheats fine with a splash of milk whisked back in.
