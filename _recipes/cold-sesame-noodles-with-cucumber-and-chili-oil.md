---
title: Cold Sesame Noodles with Cucumber and Chili Oil
date: 2026-07-27 13:46:01 -0400
description: Wheat noodles rinsed cold and coated in a peanut-sesame sauce, a starter or side that never goes near a stove after the pot drains.
image: /assets/images/Cold-Sesame-Noodles-With-Cucumber-And-Chili-Oil.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 8 min
tags: [noodles, vegetarian, quick, side, spicy, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 8 oz | dried wheat noodles
  - ¼ cup | creamy peanut butter
  - 3 tbsp | soy sauce
  - 2 tbsp | rice vinegar
  - 2 tbsp | toasted sesame oil
  - 1 tbsp | chili oil, plus more to finish
  - 1 tbsp | brown sugar
  - 2 cloves | garlic, grated
  - ¼ cup | warm water, to thin
  - 1 | cucumber, julienned
  - 3 | scallions, thinly sliced
  - To finish | toasted sesame seeds
method:
  - Boil the noodles in salted water until just tender, then drain and rinse under cold water until they've gone slick and fully cooled.
  - Whisk the peanut butter, soy sauce, rice vinegar, sesame oil, chili oil, brown sugar, and garlic until smooth, then thin with warm water until it drips off the whisk in a steady ribbon.
  - Toss the noodles with the sauce until no dry or white streaks remain.
  - Fold in the cucumber and half the scallions.
  - Pile onto a plate, scatter with the remaining scallions and sesame seeds, and finish with another drizzle of chili oil.
---

The cold rinse is doing real work, not just cooling the noodles down — it washes off the surface starch that would otherwise turn the sauce gluey within minutes. Skip it and the dish tightens into a paste before it hits the table.

No peanut butter, tahini works just as well and turns the sauce a shade more bitter, which the brown sugar was already built to balance. This holds in the fridge for a couple of days; loosen it with a splash of water before serving, since the noodles keep drinking the sauce as they sit.
