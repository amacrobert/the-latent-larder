---
title: Goan-Style Fish Curry with Coconut and Tamarind
date: 2026-07-31 05:00:02 -0400
description: A coconut curry sharp with tamarind and hot with green chilies, built around whatever firm white fish looks good that day.
image: /assets/images/Goan-Style-Fish-Curry-With-Coconut-And-Tamarind.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 25 min
tags: [fish, curry, spicy, main, quick]
ingredients:
  - 2 tbsp | coconut oil
  - 1 tsp | brown mustard seeds
  - 1 sprig | curry leaves
  - 1 | yellow onion, thinly sliced
  - 4 cloves | garlic, minced
  - 1-inch piece | ginger, minced
  - 2 | green chilies, slit
  - 1 tsp | ground turmeric
  - 1 tbsp | Kashmiri chili powder
  - 1 tsp | ground coriander
  - 14 oz can | coconut milk
  - 1 tbsp | tamarind concentrate
  - 1½ lb | white fish fillets, cut into 2-inch pieces
  - To taste | salt
  - To finish | cilantro leaves
method:
  - Warm the coconut oil in a wide pan over medium heat, then add the mustard seeds and cook until they start to pop.
  - Add the curry leaves and let them crackle and turn glassy, about 10 seconds.
  - Add the onion and cook until soft and turning gold at the edges, 6-8 minutes.
  - Stir in the garlic, ginger, and chilies; cook until fragrant, about a minute.
  - Add the turmeric, chili powder, and coriander; toast until darkened slightly and smelling toasted, about 30 seconds.
  - Pour in the coconut milk and tamarind concentrate, scraping up any browned bits, and bring to a gentle simmer.
  - Season with salt, then lower the fish into the sauce, cover, and cook until it flakes easily with a fork, 6-8 minutes.
  - Scatter cilantro over the top and serve.
---

Tamarind is what keeps this from tasting like every other coconut curry — it cuts the richness before the dish turns one-note sweet. Buy the concentrate in a jar rather than working from the whole pods; it's the same souring effect for a fraction of the effort.

Use a fish sturdy enough to survive a simmer without falling apart — snapper, cod, or halibut all hold up. Ladle it over rice so the sauce has somewhere to go.
