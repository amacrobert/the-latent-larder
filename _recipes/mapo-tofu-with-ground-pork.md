---
title: Mapo Tofu with Ground Pork
date: 2026-08-04 11:00:04 -0400
description: Silken tofu simmered in a doubanjiang-and-ground-pork sauce until it turns fire-red, ready in less time than the rice underneath it.
image: /assets/images/Mapo-Tofu-With-Ground-Pork.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 10 min
cook_time: 20 min
tags: [tofu, pork, spicy, quick, main, high-protein]
ingredients:
  - 1 lb | soft or silken tofu, cubed
  - ½ lb | ground pork
  - 2 tbsp | neutral oil
  - 3 tbsp | doubanjiang (spicy fermented bean paste)
  - 1 tbsp | fermented black beans, chopped
  - 4 cloves | garlic, minced
  - 1 tbsp | ginger, minced
  - 1 cup | chicken stock
  - 1 tbsp | soy sauce
  - 1 tsp | sugar
  - 1 tbsp | cornstarch, mixed with 2 tbsp water
  - 1 tsp | Sichuan peppercorns, toasted and ground
  - 4 | scallions, sliced
method:
  - Bring a pot of water to a bare simmer, slide in the tofu cubes, and let them sit off heat for 5 minutes to firm up and season. Drain gently.
  - Heat the oil in a wok or skillet over medium-high heat. Add the pork and break it up, cooking until it's browned and the fat has rendered, about 4 minutes.
  - Push the pork to one side, add the doubanjiang and black beans to the cleared spot, and stir until the oil around them turns red and smells toasted, about 1 minute.
  - Add the garlic and ginger, stirring until fragrant, about 30 seconds.
  - Pour in the stock, soy sauce, and sugar, then stir everything together and bring to a simmer.
  - Slide in the tofu and simmer gently, spooning sauce over the top, for 5 minutes without stirring hard enough to break the cubes apart.
  - Stir in the cornstarch slurry and simmer until the sauce clings to the tofu instead of pooling, about 1 minute.
  - Scatter the Sichuan peppercorn and scallions over the top and serve over rice.
---

The blanch is the one step that looks skippable and isn't: a minute in hot water seasons the tofu through and firms it just enough to survive simmering without turning to mush. After that, disturb the pan as little as possible — stir by shaking the pan or sliding a spatula flat along the bottom, not by chopping through the cubes.

Doubanjiang varies a lot by brand; taste the sauce before you salt anything further, since some are considerably saltier than others. Swap the pork for extra tofu or mushrooms and use vegetable stock for a vegetarian version — the doubanjiang and black beans carry enough savoriness to not miss the meat.
