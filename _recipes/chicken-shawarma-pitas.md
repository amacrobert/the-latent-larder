---
title: Chicken Shawarma Pitas with Garlic Yogurt Sauce
date: 2026-08-03 11:00:03 -0400
description: Thigh meat marinated in yogurt and warm spices, seared hard for char, then piled into pita with a garlic sauce that fights back.
image: /assets/images/Chicken-Shawarma-Pitas.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 20 min
cook_time: 15 min
tags: [chicken, sandwich, quick, main]
ingredients:
  - 1½ lb | boneless, skinless chicken thighs
  - ½ cup | plain yogurt
  - 3 tbsp | olive oil, divided
  - 2 | lemons, juiced, divided
  - 5 cloves | garlic, minced, divided
  - 2 tsp | ground cumin
  - 2 tsp | smoked paprika
  - 1 tsp | ground coriander
  - ½ tsp | ground turmeric
  - ½ tsp | ground cinnamon
  - ¼ tsp | cayenne
  - 1½ tsp | kosher salt, divided
  - ½ cup | plain yogurt, for the sauce
  - 4 | pitas, warmed
  - 1 | cucumber, thinly sliced
  - 1 | tomato, thinly sliced
  - ½ | red onion, thinly sliced
  - To finish | chopped parsley
method:
  - Whisk ½ cup yogurt, 2 tablespoons olive oil, the juice of one lemon, three-quarters of the garlic, cumin, paprika, coriander, turmeric, cinnamon, cayenne, and 1 teaspoon salt in a bowl, add the chicken, and turn to coat.
  - Marinate at least 30 minutes at room temperature, or up to overnight in the fridge.
  - Whisk the remaining garlic, remaining yogurt, remaining lemon juice, and remaining ½ teaspoon salt for the sauce and set aside.
  - Heat the remaining tablespoon of olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high until shimmering, then lay in the chicken in a single layer, working in batches if needed.
  - Sear without moving until deeply charred in spots on the first side, about 5 minutes, then flip and cook until charred and the thickest piece registers 165°F, about 5 minutes more.
  - Rest the chicken 5 minutes, then slice thin against the grain.
  - Pile the sliced chicken into warm pitas with cucumber, tomato, red onion, and parsley, and finish with the garlic yogurt sauce.
---

The yogurt marinade is doing two jobs at once — its acid tenderizes the thighs while the fat carries the spices into the meat, so don't cut the 30-minute minimum even if you're rushing. A cast iron or stainless skillet run hot and left alone is the closest a stovetop gets to a shawarma spit's char; a nonstick pan won't get you the same crust.

Chicken breast works if that's what's around, though it dries out faster, so pull it at 165°F and not a minute past. Leftover chicken is just as good cold over rice the next day, sauce spooned on top.
