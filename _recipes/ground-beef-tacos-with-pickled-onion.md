---
title: Crispy Ground Beef Tacos with Pickled Onion
date: 2026-07-31 18:30:03 -0400
description: Ground beef browned hard in a dry skillet and spiced, folded into charred tortillas with a fast-pickled onion for crunch.
image: /assets/images/Ground-Beef-Tacos-With-Pickled-Onion.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 10 min
cook_time: 15 min
tags: [beef, quick, main, spicy]
ingredients:
  - 1 | small red onion, thinly sliced
  - ¼ cup | apple cider vinegar
  - ½ tsp | sugar
  - 1 lb | ground beef (80/20)
  - 1 | small white onion, diced
  - 2 cloves | garlic, minced
  - 1 tbsp | chili powder
  - 1 tsp | ground cumin
  - ½ tsp | smoked paprika
  - ¼ cup | water
  - 8 | small corn tortillas
  - To finish | cotija, cilantro, lime wedges
method:
  - Stir the sliced red onion with the vinegar, sugar, and a pinch of salt in a small bowl and set aside to pickle while you cook the beef.
  - Heat a large dry skillet over medium-high and add the ground beef in an even layer; let it sit undisturbed for 2-3 minutes until the underside is deeply browned before breaking it up.
  - Add the diced onion and cook, stirring occasionally, until softened and the beef is cooked through, about 5 minutes.
  - Stir in the garlic, chili powder, cumin, and smoked paprika and cook until fragrant, about 30 seconds.
  - Add the water and simmer until it's mostly absorbed and the beef looks glossy rather than wet, about 2 minutes.
  - Warm the tortillas directly over a gas flame or in a dry skillet until charred in spots and pliable.
  - Fill the tortillas with the beef, top with the pickled onion, cotija, and cilantro, and serve with lime wedges.
---

The one thing that separates these from the gray, watery version: let the beef sit untouched in a hot dry pan before you break it up. That undisturbed contact is where the browning happens, and browning is where the flavor is — stir too early and you steam the meat instead of searing it.

Ground turkey works as a straight swap if you want it leaner, though it'll need a splash more fat in the pan to brown properly. The spice mix scales easily, so double it and keep the extra in a jar for the next round.
