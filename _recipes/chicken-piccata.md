---
title: Chicken Piccata with Lemon and Capers
date: 2026-08-01 20:00:03 -0400
description: Thin-pounded chicken breasts dredged and pan-fried, then finished in a bright butter-and-caper pan sauce built in the same skillet.
image: /assets/images/Chicken-Piccata-With-Lemon-And-Capers.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 20 min
tags: [chicken, quick, main]
ingredients:
  - 2 | boneless, skinless chicken breasts, halved and pounded ¼ inch thick
  - ½ cup | all-purpose flour
  - To taste | salt and pepper
  - 3 tbsp | olive oil
  - 4 tbsp | butter, divided
  - 2 cloves | garlic, minced
  - ½ cup | dry white wine
  - ½ cup | chicken broth
  - 3 tbsp | lemon juice
  - ¼ cup | capers, drained
  - A handful | parsley, chopped
method:
  - Season the chicken cutlets with salt and pepper, then dredge each in flour, shaking off the excess.
  - Heat the olive oil and 2 tablespoons of butter in a large skillet over medium-high heat until shimmering.
  - Cook the cutlets in batches until deeply golden, 3-4 minutes a side, then transfer to a plate.
  - Add the garlic to the skillet and cook until fragrant, about 30 seconds.
  - Pour in the wine, scraping up the browned bits, and let it bubble until reduced by half.
  - Stir in the broth, lemon juice, and capers, and simmer until the sauce has thickened slightly.
  - Swirl in the remaining butter off the heat until the sauce turns glossy.
  - Return the chicken to the skillet along with any collected juices, spooning sauce over top to warm through.
  - Scatter with parsley just before serving.
---

The dredge is doing more than crisping the outside — the flour that stays behind in the skillet is what thickens the sauce once the wine and broth hit it. Skip it and you've got a thin, sharp puddle instead of something that clings to the chicken.

No dry white wine on hand, use dry vermouth — it keeps longer in the cabinet anyway and nobody will taste the difference. Serve over egg noodles or with crusty bread to catch the extra sauce.
