---
title: Asparagus and Goat Cheese Quiche
date: 2026-07-29 23:30:02 -0400
description: A shortcrust custard tart, blind-baked until the bottom crisps, then filled with asparagus and goat cheese so neither turns to mush.
image: /assets/images/Asparagus-And-Goat-Cheese-Quiche.webp
servings: 6
prep_time: 35 min
cook_time: 55 min
tags: [eggs, vegetarian, breakfast, baking]
ingredients:
  - 1¼ cups | all-purpose flour
  - ½ cup | cold butter, cubed
  - ¼ tsp | salt
  - 3 tbsp | ice water
  - 1 bunch | asparagus, trimmed
  - 4 oz | goat cheese, crumbled
  - 1 | shallot, thinly sliced
  - 4 | eggs
  - ¾ cup | heavy cream
  - ½ cup | whole milk
  - A pinch | nutmeg
  - To taste | salt and pepper
method:
  - Pulse the flour, butter, and salt in a food processor until the largest pieces are pea-sized.
  - Drizzle in the ice water and pulse just until the dough clumps when pressed between your fingers.
  - Press the dough into a disk, wrap it, and chill for at least 1 hour.
  - Roll the dough into a 12-inch round, fit it into a 9-inch tart pan, and chill again for 20 minutes.
  - Line the crust with parchment and pie weights and bake at 375°F until the edges look dry, about 20 minutes.
  - Lift out the weights and parchment and bake until the bottom looks matte rather than wet, about 8 minutes more.
  - Blanch the asparagus in boiling salted water until just bright green, about 1 minute, then shock in ice water and cut into 1-inch pieces.
  - Whisk the eggs, cream, milk, nutmeg, salt, and pepper together until no streaks remain.
  - Scatter the shallot, asparagus, and goat cheese over the crust, then pour in the custard.
  - Bake at 350°F until the edges are set but the center still holds a gentle wobble, 30 to 35 minutes.
  - Cool at least 20 minutes before slicing.
---

The par-bake is what keeps this from turning into a soggy-bottomed disappointment — an unblind crust under a wet custard steams instead of crisping, and there's no fixing that after the fact. Weight it down properly and don't rush the second bake; a matte, dry bottom is the whole point.

Swap the asparagus for a handful of sautéed spinach or leek in the off-season, and any soft, tangy cheese works in place of the goat cheese — feta crumbles the same way. It holds at room temperature for hours, so it's as much a make-ahead brunch dish as a straight-from-the-oven one.
