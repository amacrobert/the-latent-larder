---
title: Steamed Char Siu Bao Buns
date: 2026-08-05 01:00:03 -0400
description: Pillowy steamed buns pleated shut around a sticky hoisin-glazed pork filling — the dim sum cart, built at home.
image: /assets/images/Char-Siu-Bao-Buns.webp
servings: 12
prep_time: 45 min (plus 1.5 hour rise)
cook_time: 15 min
tags: [pork, appetizer, steamed, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 1 cup | whole milk, warmed to 110°F
  - 2¼ tsp | active dry yeast (1 packet)
  - ¼ cup | granulated sugar
  - 3 cups | all-purpose flour, plus more for the counter
  - 2 tsp | baking powder
  - 2 tbsp | vegetable oil
  - ½ tsp | fine salt
  - 1 lb | boneless pork shoulder, diced small
  - 1 tsp | sesame oil
  - 2 cloves | garlic, minced
  - ¼ cup | hoisin sauce
  - 2 tbsp | soy sauce
  - 2 tbsp | honey
  - 1 tbsp | Shaoxing wine
  - 1 tsp | five-spice powder
  - 1 tsp | cornstarch, mixed with 2 tbsp water
  - 12 | parchment squares, for steaming
method:
  - Whisk the warm milk, yeast, and a pinch of the sugar together and let stand until foamy on top, about 5 minutes.
  - Heat the sesame oil in a skillet over medium-high and sear the pork until browned on all sides, about 6 minutes.
  - Add the garlic, hoisin, soy sauce, honey, Shaoxing wine, and five-spice, and simmer until the pork is glazed and the sauce has reduced to a syrup, about 8 minutes.
  - Stir in the cornstarch slurry and cook until the sauce thickens enough to coat the back of a spoon, about 1 minute, then let the filling cool completely.
  - Whisk the remaining sugar, flour, baking powder, and salt together, then stir in the yeast mixture and vegetable oil.
  - Knead by hand or with a dough hook until smooth and elastic, about 8 minutes, then cover and let rise at room temperature until doubled, about 1 hour.
  - Punch down the dough and divide into 12 pieces, rolling each into a ball.
  - Flatten each ball into a disc thicker in the center than at the edges, spoon in a heaping tablespoon of cooled filling, and pleat the edges closed at the top, twisting to seal.
  - Set each bun seal-side up on a parchment square, cover loosely, and let rise until visibly puffed, about 30 minutes.
  - Steam the buns in batches, spaced apart, over simmering (not boiling) water until the dough looks glossy and springs back when pressed, 12 to 15 minutes.
  - Turn off the heat and let the buns sit under the lid for 2 minutes before uncovering, then serve hot.
---

Cutting the heat and waiting those two minutes is the step that gets skipped and the one that saves the buns — lift the lid straight off high steam and the sudden temperature drop wrinkles and collapses them before they've set.

Steamed, unfilled bao dough freezes well raw: shape, first rise, then freeze on a tray before filling, and thaw before the second rise. Leftover cooked buns reheat in the same steamer for 5 minutes, or the microwave under a damp paper towel — never the oven, which turns the crumb tough instead of pillowy.
