---
title: Pan-Seared Pork Chops with Mustard Cream Sauce
date: 2026-07-28 07:46:15 -0400
description: Thick-cut chops seared hard in a screaming pan, then rested while a mustard and cream sauce comes together in the same fat.
image: /assets/images/Pan-Seared-Pork-Chops-With-Mustard-Cream-Sauce.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 10 min
cook_time: 20 min
tags: [pork, quick, main]
ingredients:
  - 4 | bone-in pork chops, 1-inch thick
  - To taste | kosher salt and black pepper
  - 2 tbsp | olive oil
  - 2 tbsp | butter
  - 2 cloves | garlic, smashed
  - A few sprigs | fresh thyme
  - ¾ cup | chicken stock
  - ¼ cup | heavy cream
  - 2 tbsp | whole-grain mustard
  - To finish | chopped parsley
method:
  - Pat the pork chops dry and season heavily with salt and pepper on both sides.
  - Heat the oil in a heavy skillet over high heat until it shimmers.
  - Lay in the chops and leave them alone until a deep brown crust forms on the first side, about 4 minutes.
  - Flip, add the butter, garlic, and thyme, and tilt the pan to spoon the foaming butter over the chops until the second side is browned and the centers read 145°F.
  - Transfer the chops to a plate to rest.
  - Pour the stock into the same pan and scrape up the browned bits until the bottom of the pan is clean.
  - Simmer until reduced by half, then whisk in the cream and mustard and cook until the sauce thickens enough to coat a spoon.
  - Return any juices from the resting plate to the pan, spoon the sauce over the chops, and finish with parsley.
---

Don't move the chops once they hit the pan — the crust that forms in those four untouched minutes is the only seasoning the sauce gets, and a pale chop makes a thin, flat one. Pull the chops at 145°F rather than cooking to gray throughout; a few minutes of resting finishes the job without drying out the meat.

Whole-grain mustard leaves texture in the sauce; swap in Dijon if you want it smooth. Spoon the whole thing over mashed potatoes or a pile of polenta so nothing gets left on the plate.
