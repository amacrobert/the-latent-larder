---
title: Potato Gratin with Thyme
date: 2026-07-26
description: Sliced potatoes baked slowly in cream until the dish turns into its own sauce.
image: /assets/images/Potato-Gratin-With-Thyme.webp
servings: 6
prep_time: 20 min
cook_time: 1 hr 15 min
tags: [potato, baking, side, vegetarian, slow]
ingredients:
  - 3 lbs | russet potatoes, thinly sliced
  - 2 cups | heavy cream
  - 1 cup | whole milk
  - 3 cloves | garlic, smashed
  - 4 sprigs | fresh thyme
  - 1½ cups | grated Gruyère
  - 2 tbsp | unsalted butter
  - ¼ tsp | freshly grated nutmeg
  - To taste | salt and black pepper
method:
  - Heat the oven to 375°F and butter a 9x13-inch baking dish.
  - Combine the cream, milk, garlic, and thyme in a saucepan and bring to a bare simmer, until steam rises and small bubbles ring the edge.
  - Season the potatoes with salt and pepper, then shingle half of them into the dish in overlapping layers.
  - Pour half the warm cream over the potatoes and scatter over half the Gruyère.
  - Layer in the rest of the potatoes, pour over the remaining cream, and top with the last of the cheese and a grating of nutmeg.
  - Dot with butter, cover tightly with foil, and bake until a knife slides into the center with no resistance, about 45 minutes.
  - Uncover and bake until the top is deeply browned and bubbling at the edges, another 20 to 30 minutes.
  - Let the gratin rest for 10 minutes before cutting, so it sets instead of running across the plate.
---

The potatoes give up their starch as they bake, and that's what turns the cream into a sauce instead of a puddle under a stack of vegetables. Slice them no thicker than a coin, and don't skip warming the cream with garlic and thyme first — it's the only seasoning that reaches the middle layers. Russets are the right choice because they break down slightly; a waxier potato holds its shape and stays gritty in the sauce.

Serve it next to the sheet-pan chicken or the grilled skirt steak, or eat a large bowl of it on its own and call that dinner.
