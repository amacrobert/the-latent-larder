---
title: Beer-Battered Onion Rings with Spicy Ranch
date: 2026-08-16 12:00:04 -0400
description: Sweet onion rings dipped in a carbonated beer batter and fried until the crust crackles — the bar-food standard, made at home.
image: /assets/images/Beer-Battered-Onion-Rings-With-Spicy-Ranch.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 15 min
tags: [appetizer, fried, vegetarian, party, quick]
ingredients:
  - 2 large | sweet onions, sliced into ½-inch rings
  - 1½ cups | all-purpose flour, divided
  - 1 tsp | baking powder
  - 1 tsp | smoked paprika
  - 1 tsp | garlic powder
  - 1 tsp | kosher salt, plus more to finish
  - ¼ tsp | cayenne pepper
  - 12 oz | cold beer, a light lager
  - For frying | vegetable oil
  - ½ cup | mayonnaise
  - 2 tbsp | buttermilk
  - 1 tbsp | hot sauce
  - 1 clove | garlic, grated
  - ¼ tsp | smoked paprika
method:
  - Separate the onion slices into individual rings, setting aside the small center pieces for another use.
  - Whisk the mayonnaise, buttermilk, hot sauce, grated garlic, and ¼ tsp smoked paprika together for the ranch and refrigerate until serving.
  - Heat 2 inches of oil in a heavy pot to 375°F.
  - Whisk 1 cup flour, baking powder, 1 tsp smoked paprika, garlic powder, salt, and cayenne in a bowl, then whisk in the cold beer until the batter is smooth and coats the back of a spoon.
  - Toss the onion rings in the remaining ½ cup flour, shaking off the excess, then dip each ring in the batter, letting the excess drip back into the bowl.
  - Fry in batches of 5 or 6 rings, without crowding the pot, until deep golden and crisp, about 2 minutes per side.
  - Drain on a wire rack and salt immediately while still hot.
  - Serve hot with the spicy ranch.
---

Pour the beer in cold, straight from the can, and don't let it go flat while you measure everything else — the carbonation is what puffs the crust, and a batter made with dead beer fries heavy instead of crackling. A light lager disappears into the batter; anything hoppier turns bitter in hot oil.

Drain on a rack, not paper towels — towels trap steam underneath and soften the bottom before you can eat them. If ranch isn't the mood, malt vinegar and a hard shake of salt does the job just as well.
