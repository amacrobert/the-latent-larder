---
title: Watermelon and Lime Granita
date: 2026-07-26
description: Frozen in a pan and raked into shards, this is dessert for a day too hot to turn on the stove.
image: /assets/images/Watermelon-And-Lime-Granita.webp
servings: 6
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 4 hrs (freezing)
tags: [dessert, fruit, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 6 cups | watermelon, cubed and seeded
  - ½ cup | granulated sugar
  - ¼ cup | lime juice
  - Pinch | salt
  - To finish | mint leaves, torn
method:
  - Puree the watermelon in a blender until completely smooth, about 30 seconds.
  - Whisk in the sugar, lime juice, and salt until the sugar has fully dissolved.
  - Pour into a metal baking pan so the liquid sits no more than an inch deep.
  - Freeze uncovered for 45 minutes, until a rim of ice crystals has formed around the edges.
  - Rake the crystals into the liquid center with a fork, breaking up any solid patches, then return to the freezer.
  - Repeat the raking every 30 to 45 minutes for about 3 more hours, until the whole pan is a loose mass of glittering ice with no liquid pooling at the bottom.
  - Scoop into chilled glasses or bowls and scatter with mint just before serving.
---

A metal pan is doing more work here than it looks like — it pulls heat out
fast enough that the sugars stay suspended in fine crystals instead of
sinking into a syrupy layer at the bottom. Glass or plastic freezes slower
and gives you a coarser, wetter granita. The other non-negotiable is the
raking schedule: skip it and walk away, and the pan sets into one solid
sheet of fruit ice that needs a chisel instead of a spoon.

Any ripe melon or soft stone fruit takes the same ratio of sugar and acid to
puree, so this is worth remembering past watermelon season. A splash of
tequila or mezcal poured over the shaved ice turns it into a drink rather
than a dessert.
