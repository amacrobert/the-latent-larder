---
title: Ribollita
date: 2026-07-29
description: Yesterday's vegetable and bean soup, thickened with stale bread until a spoon stands up in it.
image: /assets/images/Ribollita.webp
servings: 6
prep_time: 20 min
cook_time: 1 hr
tags: [soup, vegetarian, slow, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - ¼ cup | olive oil, plus more to finish
  - 1 | onion, diced
  - 2 | carrots, diced
  - 2 stalks | celery, diced
  - 4 cloves | garlic, minced
  - 1 | Parmesan rind, optional
  - 1 (28 oz) can | crushed tomatoes
  - 2 (15 oz) cans | cannellini beans, drained
  - 6 cups | vegetable broth
  - 1 bunch | Tuscan kale, stemmed and torn
  - 8 slices | day-old crusty bread, torn into chunks
  - Salt and pepper, to taste
  - To finish | olive oil and grated Parmesan
method:
  - Warm the olive oil in a large heavy pot over medium heat and cook the onion, carrots, and celery until softened, about 8 minutes.
  - Stir in the garlic and cook until fragrant, about 1 minute.
  - Add the crushed tomatoes, one can of beans, the broth, and the Parmesan rind, and bring to a simmer.
  - Purée the second can of beans with a splash of broth until smooth, then stir it into the pot — this is what gives the soup body without cream or flour.
  - Simmer uncovered until the vegetables are fully tender, about 30 minutes.
  - Stir in the kale and cook until wilted and dark, about 5 minutes.
  - Remove from the heat, fish out the Parmesan rind, and fold in the torn bread until it starts to break apart and the soup is thick enough to hold a spoon upright.
  - Let the pot sit off the heat for at least 15 minutes so the bread finishes absorbing, then season with salt and pepper and ladle into bowls.
  - Finish each bowl with a drizzle of olive oil and grated Parmesan.
---

The name means "reboiled" — this started as a way to bring leftover vegetable soup back to life with whatever stale bread was left in the box, and it's still better made a day ahead and reheated than eaten the night it's made. Puréeing half the beans instead of adding cream is what turns broth into something you'd call a stew.

Use the driest bread you can find; fresh bread turns to paste before it's had a chance to thicken anything. Cavolo nero or chard work in place of the kale if that's what's in the crisper, and a hunk of Parmesan rind — not a substitute, just a scrap saved from the last block — is the whole difference between a good pot and a forgettable one.
