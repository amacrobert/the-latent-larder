---
title: Small-Batch Strawberry Jam
date: 2026-07-29 21:04:41 -0400
description: Strawberries and sugar reduced on the stovetop to a real set — no pectin packet, no water bath, just a cold plate to tell you when to stop.
image: /assets/images/Small-Batch-Strawberry-Jam.webp
servings: 24
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 30 min (plus 1 hr macerating)
tags: [fruit, make-ahead, breakfast]
ingredients:
  - 2 lb | strawberries, hulled and quartered
  - 2 cups | sugar
  - ¼ cup | lemon juice
  - Pinch | kosher salt
method:
  - Toss the strawberries with the sugar, lemon juice, and salt in a large bowl, mashing about a third of the berries against the side to release their juice.
  - Let sit at room temperature for 1 hour, until the sugar has mostly dissolved into a thin, pink syrup.
  - Set a small plate in the freezer.
  - Pour the strawberries and their liquid into a wide, heavy pot and bring to a boil over medium-high heat, skimming off any foam.
  - Cook at a steady boil, stirring more often as it thickens, until the fruit has broken down and the bubbles have gone slow and heavy, 20 to 25 minutes.
  - Drop a spoonful onto the frozen plate and drag a finger through it — if the trail holds its edge instead of flooding back in, it's set; if not, boil another few minutes and test again.
  - Ladle into clean jars while still hot, leaving a half-inch of headspace, and let cool uncovered on the counter before sealing.
  - Refrigerate once cool.
---

The set here comes from reduction and the lemon juice's acid, not added pectin, so don't rush the boil — a thin jam just means it needed more time, not more sugar. The cold-plate test is more reliable than the clock; strawberries vary enough in natural pectin that two batches rarely hit the set point at the same minute.

It keeps a month in the fridge unprocessed, or up to a year if you process the jars in a water bath. Good on a warm biscuit, or swirled into pancake batter just before it hits the griddle.
