---
title: Swedish Meatballs in Cream Sauce
date: 2026-08-03 05:00:03 -0400
description: Pan-browned beef and pork meatballs finished in a nutmeg-spiced cream gravy, spooned over egg noodles with lingonberry on the side.
image: /assets/images/Swedish-Meatballs-In-Cream-Sauce.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 25 min
tags: [beef, pork, main, quick]
ingredients:
  - 1 lb | ground beef
  - ½ lb | ground pork
  - ½ cup | dry breadcrumbs
  - ¼ cup | whole milk
  - 1 | egg
  - 1 | small onion, grated
  - ½ tsp | ground allspice
  - ¼ tsp | ground nutmeg
  - To taste | salt and pepper
  - 3 tbsp | unsalted butter, divided
  - 3 tbsp | all-purpose flour
  - 2 cups | beef stock
  - ½ cup | heavy cream
  - 1 tbsp | soy sauce
  - 1 tsp | Dijon mustard
  - 8 oz | egg noodles
  - To finish | lingonberry jam and chopped dill
method:
  - Soak the breadcrumbs in the milk until softened, about 5 minutes.
  - Mix the soaked breadcrumbs with the beef, pork, egg, grated onion, allspice, nutmeg, salt, and pepper by hand until just combined.
  - Roll into 20 meatballs, about 1 inch each.
  - Melt 2 tbsp of the butter in a large skillet over medium-high and brown the meatballs on all sides, working in batches, about 8 minutes a batch; transfer to a plate.
  - Bring a pot of salted water to a boil and cook the egg noodles until tender, then drain.
  - Melt the remaining 1 tbsp butter in the same skillet and whisk in the flour, cooking until golden and fragrant, about 1 minute.
  - Whisk in the beef stock a little at a time until smooth, then stir in the cream, soy sauce, and Dijon and bring to a simmer.
  - Nestle the meatballs back into the sauce and simmer until cooked through and the sauce has thickened enough to coat the back of a spoon, about 10 minutes.
  - Serve over the egg noodles with lingonberry jam and dill.
---

The soy sauce is the trick here — it's not in a traditional Swedish kitchen, but it does the same job that a long-simmered stock would, pushing the gravy toward savory without muddying the nutmeg and allspice. Don't skip the grated (not diced) onion in the meatballs; it melts into the mix instead of leaving pockets of raw bite.

No lingonberry on hand, cranberry sauce works as a stand-in — you want that same tart contrast against the rich gravy, not a substitute that tries to be sweet.
