---
title: Jamaican-Style Curried Goat
date: 2026-07-30 10:30:03 -0400
description: Bone-in goat marinated in curry powder, seared hard, then given two and a half hours in a spiced braise until it falls from the bone.
image: /assets/images/Jamaican-Style-Curried-Goat.webp
servings: 6
prep_time: 20 min (plus 1 hr marinate)
cook_time: 2 hr 30 min
tags: [goat, curry, slow, spicy, main]
ingredients:
  - 3 lb | bone-in goat shoulder, cut into 2-inch pieces
  - 3 tbsp | curry powder, divided
  - 2 tbsp | vegetable oil
  - 1 | yellow onion, chopped
  - 4 cloves | garlic, minced
  - 1 tbsp | fresh ginger, grated
  - 1 | scotch bonnet pepper, pierced once
  - 2 sprigs | fresh thyme
  - ¼ tsp | ground allspice
  - 2 cups | chicken stock
  - 1 lb | potatoes, peeled and cut into chunks
  - To taste | salt and black pepper
  - To finish | sliced scallion
method:
  - Toss the goat with 2 tbsp curry powder, salt, and pepper, then let it sit at room temperature for 1 hour so the spice has time to work into the meat.
  - Heat the oil in a Dutch oven over high heat and sear the goat in batches until deeply browned on at least two sides, about 8 minutes per batch, then set aside.
  - Add the onion to the pot and cook until softened and browned at the edges, about 6 minutes.
  - Stir in the garlic, ginger, and remaining 1 tbsp curry powder and cook until fragrant, about 1 minute.
  - Return the goat to the pot along with the scotch bonnet, thyme, allspice, and stock, then bring to a boil, cover, and drop to a low simmer.
  - Braise until the meat is tender enough to pull from the bone but still holding together, about 2 hours, checking the liquid level occasionally.
  - Add the potatoes and continue simmering uncovered until they're tender and the sauce coats the back of a spoon, about 30 minutes.
  - Discard the scotch bonnet and thyme sprigs, taste and adjust the salt, and finish with scallion.
---

Marinating the goat in curry powder before it goes anywhere near the pot is what separates this from a stew dusted with spice at the end — the powder needs contact time with raw meat to bloom into something rounder than what a few minutes in hot oil can pull out of it. Sear in batches and don't rush it; a crowded pot steams instead of browns, and the browned bits left behind are most of the dish's flavor.

Goat can be hard to track down outside a halal or Caribbean market — bone-in lamb shoulder works as a stand-in, though it cooks faster and softer, so start checking around 90 minutes in. Serve over white rice with the sauce spooned on generously; it also keeps and reheats better the next day, once the spices have had time to settle in.
