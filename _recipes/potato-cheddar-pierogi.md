---
title: Pan-Fried Potato and Cheddar Pierogi
date: 2026-08-12 18:00:03 -0400
description: Hand-pleated dumplings boiled until they float, then finished in butter and onions until the edges blister.
image: /assets/images/Potato-Cheddar-Pierogi.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 1 hour
cook_time: 20 min
tags: [potato, cheese, vegetarian, main, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 2 cups | all-purpose flour, plus more for rolling
  - ½ tsp | Kosher salt
  - 1 | large egg
  - ¼ cup | sour cream, plus more to serve
  - ½ cup | warm water
  - 1 lb | russet potatoes, peeled and quartered
  - ¾ cup | sharp cheddar, shredded
  - 1 | small onion, finely diced
  - 4 tbsp | butter, divided
  - To taste | salt and black pepper
method:
  - Whisk the egg, sour cream, and warm water together, then stir into the flour and salt until a shaggy dough forms.
  - Turn the dough out and knead until smooth and elastic, about 5 minutes, then wrap and rest at room temperature for 30 minutes.
  - Boil the potatoes in salted water until a knife slides in with no resistance, about 15 minutes, then drain.
  - Melt 2 tablespoons of the butter in a skillet and cook half the onion over medium heat until soft and translucent, about 5 minutes.
  - Mash the potatoes with the cheddar and the cooked onion, season with salt and pepper, and let the filling cool until it's no longer steaming.
  - Roll the dough out thin on a floured surface and cut into 3-inch rounds.
  - Spoon a teaspoon of filling onto each round, fold in half, and pinch the edges tightly closed, crimping with a fork.
  - Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and drop the pierogi in batches, cooking until they float to the surface, then 2 minutes more.
  - Lift the pierogi out with a slotted spoon and let them steam-dry on a towel-lined tray.
  - Melt the remaining butter in a skillet with the remaining onion and pan-fry the boiled pierogi in batches until golden and blistered on both sides.
  - Serve hot with extra sour cream.
---

The dough should feel like play dough — tacky but not sticking to your hands — and the rest is what keeps it from snapping back when you roll it. Don't skip the boil before the pan-fry: it cooks the dough through, and the butter step is only there to crisp and brown what's already done.

Leftover boiled pierogi keep in the fridge for a few days or freeze on a tray before bagging — pan-fry straight from frozen, no thawing needed. A spoonful of sauerkraut folded into the filling is the classic variation if you want something sharper than plain potato and cheddar.
