---
title: Skillet Chicken Pot Pie with Flaky Crust
date: 2026-07-31 16:00:08 -0400
description: A stovetop roux and rotisserie chicken under a puff pastry lid, baked in the same skillet the filling was built in.
image: /assets/images/Chicken-Pot-Pie.webp
servings: 6
prep_time: 25 min
cook_time: 45 min
tags: [chicken, baking, main, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 4 tbsp | unsalted butter
  - 1 | small onion, diced
  - 2 | carrots, diced
  - 2 stalks | celery, diced
  - 3 cloves | garlic, minced
  - 5 tbsp | all-purpose flour
  - 2 cups | chicken broth
  - ¾ cup | heavy cream
  - 3 cups | shredded cooked chicken
  - 1 cup | frozen peas
  - 1 tsp | fresh thyme leaves
  - Salt and pepper | to taste
  - 1 sheet | puff pastry, thawed
  - 1 | egg, beaten
method:
  - Melt the butter in a large oven-safe skillet over medium heat and cook the onion, carrots, and celery until the onion turns translucent, about 6 minutes.
  - Stir in the garlic and cook until fragrant, about 30 seconds.
  - Sprinkle the flour over the vegetables and stir constantly for 2 minutes, until it smells toasted and coats everything like wet sand.
  - Whisk in the chicken broth and cream, scraping up anything stuck to the pan, and simmer until the sauce thickens enough to coat the back of a spoon.
  - Stir in the chicken, peas, and thyme, season with salt and pepper, and pull the skillet off the heat.
  - Drape the puff pastry over the skillet, tuck the excess down against the inside edge, and cut two or three vents in the top.
  - Brush the pastry with the egg wash and bake at 400°F until the top is deep golden and the filling bubbles up through the vents, 25 to 30 minutes.
  - Let the pie rest 10 minutes before cutting in — the filling is lava straight out of the oven.
---

The roux is the part worth paying attention to. Walk away before the flour has cooked its full two minutes and you get a pasty sauce that never quite thickens; keep stirring until it smells faintly toasted before the broth goes in. Rotisserie chicken is the shortcut worth taking — it also means the filling comes together in the same twenty minutes as the vegetables.

No pie dish required: the skillet the filling was built in goes straight into the oven under the pastry lid. Swap in a round of standard pie dough if that's what's in the freezer — roll it just larger than the skillet and press it against the sides the same way you would a crust.
