---
title: Ground Beef Enchiladas with Red Chile Gravy
date: 2026-08-09 09:00:03 -0400
description: Browned ground beef and onion rolled into corn tortillas under a from-scratch chili-powder gravy, baked until the edges crisp.
image: /assets/images/Ground-Beef-Enchiladas.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 20 min
cook_time: 30 min
tags: [beef, baking, main, cheese]
ingredients:
  - 3 tbsp | vegetable oil
  - 3 tbsp | flour
  - 3 tbsp | chili powder
  - 2 cups | beef broth
  - 1 tbsp | tomato paste
  - 1 tsp | ground cumin
  - 1 lb | ground beef (80/20)
  - 1 | small onion, diced
  - 3 cloves | garlic, minced
  - 8 oz | shredded cheddar
  - 12 | corn tortillas
  - To finish | sliced scallions and sour cream
method:
  - Whisk the oil and flour together in a saucepan over medium heat and cook, whisking, until the paste turns a shade darker and smells toasty, about 2 minutes.
  - Whisk in the chili powder and cook another 30 seconds, then slowly whisk in the broth until smooth.
  - Stir in the tomato paste and cumin, bring to a simmer, and cook until the gravy coats the back of a spoon, about 8 minutes. Season with salt and set aside.
  - Brown the ground beef in a skillet over medium-high, breaking it up, until no pink remains, about 7 minutes; drain excess fat.
  - Add the onion and cook until softened, about 5 minutes, then stir in the garlic and cook until fragrant, 30 seconds.
  - Off the heat, stir a ladle of the red chile gravy and half the cheese into the beef until the filling holds together.
  - Spread a thin layer of the gravy over the bottom of a 9x13-inch baking dish.
  - Warm the tortillas until pliable, fill each with the beef mixture, roll tightly, and lay seam-side down in the dish.
  - Pour the remaining gravy over the top and scatter with the rest of the cheese.
  - Bake at 400°F until the gravy bubbles at the edges and the cheese is browned in spots, about 20 minutes.
  - Rest 5 minutes, then finish with scallions and sour cream before serving.
---

The gravy is the whole recipe — building it from a roux and chili powder rather than reaching for a can gives it a rounder, less metallic heat, and toasting the flour-and-chili paste before the broth goes in is what keeps it from tasting raw. Don't skip draining the beef; excess fat on top of a already-rich gravy turns the dish greasy instead of saucy.

Swap in ground turkey for a leaner version, or use the leftover gravy on huevos rancheros the next morning — it keeps in the fridge for a few days and only gets better.
