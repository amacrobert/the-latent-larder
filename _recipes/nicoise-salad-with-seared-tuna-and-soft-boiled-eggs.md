---
title: Niçoise Salad with Seared Tuna and Soft-Boiled Eggs
date: 2026-07-30 19:00:03 -0400
description: A composed, not tossed, summer main — each component cooked on its own and arranged, never dressed until the plate is built.
image: /assets/images/Nicoise-Salad-With-Seared-Tuna-And-Soft-Boiled-Eggs.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 20 min
cook_time: 15 min
tags: [salad, fish, quick, main]
ingredients:
  - 2 (6 oz) | tuna steaks
  - 4 | eggs
  - ½ lb | baby potatoes
  - ½ lb | green beans, trimmed
  - 1 cup | cherry tomatoes, halved
  - ½ cup | Niçoise olives
  - 2 tbsp | capers
  - 2 | anchovy fillets, minced
  - 1 clove | garlic, minced
  - 2 tbsp | red wine vinegar
  - 4 tbsp | olive oil, divided
  - Salt and pepper | to taste
method:
  - Boil the potatoes in salted water until a knife slides in with no resistance, about 12 minutes, then drain and halve while still warm.
  - Blanch the green beans in the same pot until bright green and just tender, about 2 minutes, then shock in ice water and drain.
  - Lower the eggs into simmering water and cook exactly 6 minutes for a soft-set yolk, then transfer to ice water and peel.
  - Whisk the garlic, anchovy, vinegar, and 3 tablespoons olive oil into a dressing; season with salt and pepper.
  - Pat the tuna dry, season well, and sear in the remaining oil over high heat, about 90 seconds per side, until crusted outside and still rare at the center.
  - Slice the tuna against the grain and arrange it with the potatoes, green beans, tomatoes, olives, and halved eggs on a platter, then spoon the dressing over just before serving.
---

The whole dish falls apart if the tuna and eggs go past where they're supposed to stop — pull the tuna while the center is still red, and don't let the eggs sit in the hot water a minute longer than six, or you lose the soft yolk that's meant to run into the dressing. Keep the components separate on the platter rather than tossing them together; it's what makes this a composed salad instead of a bowl of leftovers.

No stove required if you'd rather skip the sear: good tuna packed in olive oil, flaked over the same vegetables, is a legitimate version of this salad and the one most often served in Nice itself.
