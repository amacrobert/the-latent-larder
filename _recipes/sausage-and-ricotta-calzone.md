---
title: Sausage and Ricotta Calzone
date: 2026-08-04 10:00:03 -0400
description: A folded, sealed pizza that eats like a hot pocket with better bread — crisp outside, molten ricotta and sausage inside.
image: /assets/images/Sausage-And-Ricotta-Calzone.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 25 min (plus 1½ hr rise)
cook_time: 20 min
tags: [pizza, pork, baking, main, slow, cheese]
ingredients:
  - 3½ cups | bread flour, plus more for the counter
  - 1¼ cups | warm water (110°F)
  - 2 tsp | active dry yeast
  - 1 tbsp | sugar
  - 2 tsp | fine salt
  - 2 tbsp | olive oil, plus more for the bowl and brushing
  - 12 oz | Italian sausage, casings removed
  - 15 oz | whole-milk ricotta
  - 8 oz | low-moisture mozzarella, shredded
  - ¼ cup | grated Parmesan
  - 1 | egg, beaten
  - 1 tsp | dried oregano
  - To finish | crushed tomatoes, warmed, for dipping
method:
  - Whisk the warm water, yeast, and sugar together and let stand until foamy on top, about 5 minutes.
  - Stir in the flour, salt, and olive oil, then knead by hand or with a dough hook until smooth and elastic, about 8 minutes.
  - Transfer to an oiled bowl, cover, and let rise at room temperature until doubled, about 1½ hours.
  - Meanwhile, brown the sausage in a skillet over medium-high heat, breaking it into small pieces, until no pink remains, about 6 minutes, then let cool.
  - Stir together the ricotta, mozzarella, Parmesan, oregano, and cooled sausage; season with salt and pepper.
  - Set a pizza steel or stone on the top oven rack and heat the oven as high as it goes, at least 500°F, for 45 minutes before baking.
  - Punch down the dough and divide it into four balls; let rest, covered, for 15 minutes.
  - On a floured counter, stretch one ball into a 9-inch round, then spoon a quarter of the filling onto one half, leaving a bare border.
  - Fold the dough over the filling into a half-moon and crimp the edge tightly, pressing out any air pockets, then transfer to a cornmeal-dusted peel.
  - Brush the top with beaten egg and cut two small steam vents, then slide onto the hot steel and bake until deeply browned and blistered, 12 to 15 minutes.
  - Let rest 5 minutes before cutting open — the ricotta stays lava-hot longer than the crust suggests. Repeat with the remaining dough and filling.
  - Serve with warmed crushed tomatoes on the side for dipping.
---

The crimp matters more than the stretch here — a thin spot or a loose seam is where the filling boils out and scorches on the steel. Press the edge with a fork if your fingers aren't sealing it fully shut, and don't overfill; a slightly stingy hand with the ricotta is what keeps the fold closed.

Leftover calzones reheat better than the pizza does: 10 minutes at 450°F on a rack brings the crust back to crisp without drying out the filling, where a microwave turns the crust to rubber.
