---
title: Crispy Instant Pot Pork Carnitas
date: 2026-07-31 12:00:03 -0400
description: Pork shoulder pressure-cooked meltingly tender in under an hour, then blasted under the broiler for the crisp, caramelized edges carnitas are supposed to have.
image: /assets/images/Instant-Pot-Pork-Carnitas.webp
servings: 6
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 75 min
tags: [pork, main, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 3 lb | pork shoulder, cut into 2-inch chunks
  - 1 tbsp | kosher salt
  - 1 tsp | ground cumin
  - 1 tsp | dried oregano
  - ½ tsp | ground cinnamon
  - 2 tbsp | vegetable oil
  - 4 cloves | garlic, smashed
  - 1 | white onion, quartered
  - 1 | orange, juiced, peel reserved
  - ¼ cup | lime juice
  - 1 cup | chicken broth
  - 2 | bay leaves
  - Corn tortillas, diced onion, cilantro, lime wedges | to serve
method:
  - Season the pork chunks all over with the salt, cumin, oregano, and cinnamon.
  - Set the Instant Pot to sauté, heat the oil, and brown the pork in batches until deeply colored on at least two sides, about 8 minutes a batch.
  - Return all the pork to the pot with the garlic, onion, orange juice and reserved peel, lime juice, broth, and bay leaves.
  - Seal the lid and cook on high pressure for 45 minutes, then let the pressure release naturally for 15 minutes before opening.
  - Lift the pork out, discard the bay leaves and orange peel, and shred the meat with two forks, moistening it with a few spoonfuls of the cooking liquid.
  - Spread the shredded pork on a sheet pan, spoon some of the fat skimmed off the liquid over the top, and broil until the edges crisp and char in spots, 5 to 8 minutes.
  - Pile onto warm tortillas with onion, cilantro, and lime.
---

The broiler is doing the job a three-hour simmer in lard usually does — the pressure cooker gets the meat fall-apart tender in under an hour, and a few minutes under high heat gives the shredded edges the same crackle and char without a pot to babysit all afternoon.

Skim the fat rather than pouring it off; it's what crisps under the broiler and carries most of the flavor. Leftovers reheat by going straight back under the broiler for a minute — they crisp up better the second time than most things do.
