---
title: Penne alla Vodka with Pancetta
date: 2026-08-06 15:00:02 -0400
description: A tomato-cream sauce built on rendered pancetta and a splash of vodka, which cooks off but leaves the sauce silkier than tomato and cream alone.
image: /assets/images/Penne-Alla-Vodka.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 10 min
cook_time: 25 min
tags: [pasta, main, quick, pork]
ingredients:
  - 12 oz | penne
  - 2 tbsp | olive oil
  - 4 oz | pancetta, diced
  - 1 | shallot, minced
  - 3 cloves | garlic, minced
  - ¼ cup | tomato paste
  - ¼ cup | vodka
  - 28 oz can | crushed tomatoes
  - ½ tsp | red pepper flakes
  - ½ cup | heavy cream
  - ½ cup | grated Parmesan, plus more to finish
  - To finish | torn basil
  - To taste | salt and black pepper
method:
  - Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil and cook the penne until just shy of al dente, then reserve 1 cup pasta water and drain.
  - Meanwhile, heat the olive oil in a large skillet over medium heat and cook the pancetta, stirring occasionally, until crisp and browned, about 6 minutes.
  - Add the shallot and cook until softened, about 3 minutes, then stir in the garlic and cook until fragrant, 30 seconds.
  - Stir in the tomato paste and cook, stirring, until it darkens a shade and coats the bottom of the pan, about 2 minutes.
  - Pour in the vodka, scraping up the browned bits, and simmer until it's reduced by half and you can no longer smell raw alcohol, about 3 minutes.
  - Add the crushed tomatoes and red pepper flakes and simmer, stirring occasionally, until slightly thickened, about 10 minutes.
  - Stir in the cream and Parmesan until the sauce turns pale orange and glossy.
  - Add the drained penne to the skillet and toss, loosening with splashes of pasta water until the sauce clings to the noodles.
  - Season with salt and pepper and scatter with basil before serving.
---

The vodka isn't a flavor so much as a tool — it dissolves flavor compounds in the tomato paste that water and fat can't reach on their own, which is why the sauce tastes rounder than a plain tomato-cream base. Let it actually simmer down; a sauce that still smells boozy hasn't finished the job.

Swap the pancetta for guanciale if you have it, or leave it out and start the sauce in olive oil for a vegetarian version — the tomato paste and cream still carry it.
