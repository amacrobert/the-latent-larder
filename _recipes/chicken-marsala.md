---
title: Skillet Chicken Marsala with Cremini Mushrooms
date: 2026-08-05 03:00:04 -0400
description: Dredged chicken cutlets seared hard, then simmered in a Marsala wine and mushroom pan sauce reduced until glossy.
image: /assets/images/Chicken-Marsala.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 25 min
tags: [chicken, quick, main]
ingredients:
  - 4 | chicken breast cutlets, pounded ¼ inch thick
  - ½ cup | all-purpose flour
  - Salt and pepper | to taste
  - 3 tbsp | olive oil
  - 4 tbsp | unsalted butter, divided
  - 8 oz | cremini mushrooms, sliced
  - 2 cloves | garlic, minced
  - ¾ cup | dry Marsala wine
  - ¾ cup | chicken stock
  - 1 tsp | fresh thyme leaves
  - To finish | chopped parsley
method:
  - Season the cutlets with salt and pepper, then dredge in flour, shaking off the excess.
  - Heat the olive oil and 2 tbsp butter in a large skillet over medium-high until the butter foams.
  - Sear the cutlets in batches until deep golden brown, 3 to 4 minutes per side, then transfer to a plate.
  - Add the remaining 2 tbsp butter and the mushrooms to the skillet and cook, stirring occasionally, until well browned and the liquid they release has evaporated, 6 to 8 minutes.
  - Stir in the garlic and thyme and cook until fragrant, about 30 seconds.
  - Pour in the Marsala, scraping up the browned bits, and simmer until reduced by half, about 3 minutes.
  - Add the chicken stock and return the chicken and any collected juices to the skillet, then simmer until the sauce coats the back of a spoon and the chicken is cooked through, about 5 minutes.
  - Scatter with parsley and serve straight from the skillet.
---

Sear the mushrooms harder than feels natural — they need to fully brown and dry out before the wine goes in, or the sauce turns watery instead of glossy. Use a dry Marsala, not the sweet cream-sherry style sold as a cooking wine; the dry version reduces to something savory instead of syrupy.

Serve over egg noodles or mashed potatoes to catch the sauce. No Marsala on hand: dry vermouth gets you most of the way there, though you'll lose the caramel edge.
