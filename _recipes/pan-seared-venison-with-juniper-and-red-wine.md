---
title: Pan-Seared Venison with Juniper and Red Wine
date: 2026-07-30 04:00:03 -0400
description: A hard sear, a short rest, and a juniper-spiked wine reduction — the fastest way to make game taste like a special occasion instead of a mistake.
image: /assets/images/Pan-Seared-Venison-With-Juniper-And-Red-Wine.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 15 min
tags: [venison, quick, main]
ingredients:
  - 1½ lb | venison loin, trimmed
  - 2 tbsp | olive oil
  - 3 tbsp | butter, divided
  - 2 cloves | garlic, smashed
  - 6 | juniper berries, crushed
  - 2 sprigs | thyme
  - 1 | shallot, minced
  - 1 cup | dry red wine
  - 1 cup | beef stock
  - 1 tbsp | red currant jelly
  - Salt and pepper | to taste
method:
  - Pat the venison dry, season it heavily with salt and pepper, and let it sit at room temperature for 20 minutes.
  - Heat the olive oil in a heavy skillet over high heat until it just starts to shimmer.
  - Sear the venison on all sides until a deep brown crust forms, about 2 minutes per side.
  - Add 1 tbsp butter, the garlic, thyme, and juniper berries, and spoon the foaming butter over the meat for a minute, then move it to a plate to rest.
  - Lower the heat to medium, add the shallot to the same pan, and cook until softened and translucent.
  - Pour in the red wine, scraping up the browned bits, and simmer until reduced by half.
  - Add the stock and currant jelly and simmer until the sauce coats the back of a spoon.
  - Off the heat, swirl in the remaining butter until glossy, then slice the venison against the grain and spoon the sauce over the top.
---

Venison loin has almost no fat to protect it, so the sear is the whole recipe — get the pan properly hot, don't crowd it, and pull the meat at 125°F for a rosy medium-rare. Anything past medium turns it into shoe leather with a grudge.

No juniper berries on hand, crush a couple of gin-soaked ice cubes' worth of gin instead, or leave them out entirely — the wine and currant jelly still get you most of the way there. This is good with the [potato gratin](/recipes/potato-gratin-with-thyme/) or a pile of buttered egg noodles to catch the sauce.
