title: Dutch Oven Beef Stew with Carrots and Potatoes
date: 2026-08-01 23:00:03 -0400
description: Cubed chuck browned hard and simmered low in a flour-thickened broth until a spoon is the only tool the bowl needs.
image: /assets/images/Dutch-Oven-Beef-Stew.webp
servings: 6
prep_time: 20 min
cook_time: 2 hrs 30 min
tags: [beef, slow, main, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 2 lb | beef chuck, cut into 1½-inch cubes
  - To taste | kosher salt and black pepper
  - ¼ cup | all-purpose flour
  - 3 tbsp | neutral oil
  - 1 | onion, diced
  - 2 stalks | celery, sliced
  - 3 cloves | garlic, minced
  - 2 tbsp | tomato paste
  - 1 cup | dry red wine
  - 4 cups | beef stock
  - 1 tbsp | Worcestershire sauce
  - 2 | bay leaves
  - 4 sprigs | thyme
  - 4 | carrots, cut into chunks
  - 1 lb | Yukon gold potatoes, cut into chunks
  - 1 cup | frozen peas
  - To finish | chopped parsley
method:
  - Toss the beef cubes with a heavy pinch of salt and pepper, then dust with the flour until lightly coated.
  - Heat 2 tablespoons of the oil in a Dutch oven over high heat and brown the beef in batches, about 3 minutes per side, without crowding the pot, then set aside.
  - Add the remaining oil, onion, and celery to the pot and cook until the onion turns translucent, about 5 minutes.
  - Stir in the garlic and tomato paste and cook until fragrant, about 1 minute.
  - Pour in the wine, scraping up the browned bits from the bottom, and simmer until reduced by half.
  - Return the beef to the pot along with the stock, Worcestershire, bay leaves, and thyme. Bring to a simmer, cover, and cook in a 325°F oven until the beef is just short of tender, about 1½ hours.
  - Stir in the carrots and potatoes, cover, and continue cooking until the vegetables and beef are both fork-tender, about 45 minutes more.
  - Stir in the peas and cook until bright green and heated through, about 5 minutes.
  - Discard the bay leaves and thyme stems, taste and adjust the salt, and finish with parsley.
---

The flour dredge before searing is what separates a stew from a pot roast with extra liquid — it browns into the fond and thickens the broth as it simmers, so there's no separate roux to whisk in later. Sear the beef hard and in batches; a crowded pot steams the meat instead of browning it, and that fond is most of the flavor in the finished broth.

This keeps well and improves overnight in the fridge — reheat gently on the stove, adding a splash of stock if the gravy's tightened up too much.
