---
title: Herb-Crusted Prime Rib with Horseradish Cream
date: 2026-07-30 23:00:03 -0400
description: A whole rib roast salted two days ahead and reverse-seared, so the crust arrives in the last ten minutes instead of the first three hours.
image: /assets/images/Herb-Crusted-Prime-Rib-With-Horseradish-Cream.webp
servings: 8
prep_time: 20 min (plus 2-day dry brine)
cook_time: 3 hr
tags: [beef, roasted, slow, main]
ingredients:
  - 1 (4-bone) | prime rib roast
  - 3 tbsp | kosher salt
  - 4 cloves | garlic, minced
  - 2 tbsp | fresh rosemary, chopped
  - 2 tbsp | fresh thyme, chopped
  - ¼ cup | olive oil
  - 2 tbsp | black pepper, cracked
  - ½ cup | sour cream
  - ¼ cup | prepared horseradish
  - 1 tbsp | lemon juice
  - To taste | salt
method:
  - Two days ahead, salt the roast heavily on all sides and set it uncovered on a rack in the fridge, so the surface dries out enough to brown properly.
  - An hour before cooking, pull the roast out to lose its chill.
  - Mash the garlic, rosemary, thyme, and olive oil into a paste and pack it over every surface, then crack the pepper on top so it beads into the paste instead of falling off.
  - Roast bone-side down at 250°F until an instant-read thermometer reads 115°F at the center, about 3 hours for a 4-bone roast.
  - Pull the roast and rest it loosely tented for 20 minutes — the center will climb another 5 to 10 degrees on its own.
  - Raise the oven to 500°F and whisk the sour cream, horseradish, lemon juice, and a pinch of salt while it heats.
  - Slide the roast back in for 8 to 10 minutes, until the crust has gone deep brown and crackling.
  - Carve against the grain into thick slices and serve with the horseradish cream.
---

The two-day salt does the same job here as it does on a smoked brisket or a whole roasted duck: it draws moisture to the surface and lets it dry off, so the outside has something to brown instead of steaming under a wet crust. Cooking low first and hot at the very end — instead of the usual sear-then-roast — is what keeps the whole roast an even pink edge to edge, with the crust confined to where you actually want it.

A meat thermometer isn't optional here; a roast this size will keep climbing well past 115°F if you pull it late; catch it at 115°F for the final rise to land you at a true medium-rare. Leftover slices, if there are any, are better cold on a sandwich the next day than reheated.
