---
title: Crumb-Topped Blueberry Muffins
date: 2026-07-31 23:00:02 -0400
description: Domed muffins started in a hot oven so they rise before the blueberries can drag them down, finished with a crunchy streusel crust.
image: /assets/images/Crumb-Topped-Blueberry-Muffins.webp
servings: 12
prep_time: 20 min
cook_time: 22 min
tags: [breakfast, baking, vegetarian, fruit, make-ahead]
ingredients:
  - 2 cups | all-purpose flour, divided
  - 1 cup | sugar
  - 2 tsp | baking powder
  - ½ tsp | salt
  - ½ cup | unsalted butter, melted
  - 2 | eggs
  - ¾ cup | buttermilk
  - 1 tsp | vanilla extract
  - 1½ cups | blueberries, fresh or frozen
  - ¼ cup | sugar, for the streusel
  - ¼ tsp | cinnamon
  - 3 tbsp | cold unsalted butter, cubed, for the streusel
method:
  - Preheat the oven to 425°F and line a 12-cup muffin tin with paper liners.
  - Make the streusel by rubbing the ¼ cup sugar, cinnamon, cold butter, and 3 tablespoons of the flour between your fingers until it forms coarse, pea-sized clumps, then chill it while you make the batter.
  - Whisk together the remaining flour, 1 cup sugar, baking powder, and salt in a large bowl.
  - In a separate bowl, whisk the melted butter, eggs, buttermilk, and vanilla until smooth.
  - Toss the blueberries with a spoonful of the flour mixture to coat.
  - Pour the wet ingredients into the dry and fold just until no streaks of flour remain, then fold in the blueberries — the batter should be lumpy, not smooth.
  - Divide the batter among the muffin cups, mounding it well above the rim, and scatter the streusel over the tops.
  - Bake at 425°F for 5 minutes, then, without opening the door, lower the oven to 350°F and continue baking until the tops are golden and a skewer comes out clean, 16 to 18 minutes more.
  - Cool in the tin for 5 minutes, then transfer to a rack to cool the rest of the way.
---

The hot start is what gives these a real bakery dome instead of a flat top — the initial blast sets the edges and pushes the center up before the batter has a chance to spread. Don't skip the flour toss on the blueberries either; it's the only thing standing between you and a muffin with all the fruit sunk in the bottom half.

Frozen blueberries go in straight from the freezer, no thawing — thawed ones bleed purple through the whole batter. Swap in raspberries or diced peach in the same amount if that's what's around, and the streusel keeps in the freezer for a couple weeks if you want to double a batch ahead.
