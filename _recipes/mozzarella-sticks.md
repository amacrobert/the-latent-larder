---
title: Crispy Mozzarella Sticks with Marinara
date: 2026-08-13 06:00:05 -0400
description: Low-moisture mozzarella double-breaded, frozen solid, then fried hot and fast so the crust sets before the cheese can blow out.
image: /assets/images/Crispy-Mozzarella-Sticks-With-Marinara.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 20 min (plus 1 hour freezing)
cook_time: 10 min
tags: [appetizer, cheese, fried, quick, vegetarian]
ingredients:
  - 12 | mozzarella string cheese sticks
  - ¾ cup | all-purpose flour
  - 2 | eggs
  - 1 tbsp | water
  - 1½ cups | panko breadcrumbs
  - 1 tsp | dried oregano
  - 1 tsp | garlic powder
  - ½ tsp | salt
  - For frying | vegetable oil
  - 1 cup | marinara sauce, warmed, to serve
method:
  - Whisk the eggs with the water in one bowl. Put the flour in a second bowl, and stir the panko, oregano, garlic powder, and salt together in a third.
  - Roll each cheese stick through the flour, then the egg, then press firmly into the panko until fully coated. Dip back through the egg and panko a second time so there are no bare patches.
  - Arrange the breaded sticks in a single layer on a parchment-lined tray and freeze at least 1 hour, until solid all the way through.
  - Pour oil into a heavy pot to a depth of 2 inches and heat to 350°F.
  - Fry the frozen sticks in small batches, turning once, until deep golden brown, about 90 seconds a side — pull them the moment the crust colors, before the cheese has a chance to split the coating.
  - Drain on a wire rack and serve immediately with the warm marinara.
---

The freeze is the whole recipe: bread them straight from the fridge and the cheese melts before the crust sets, so you get a puddle in the fryer instead of a stick. Frozen solid, the outside can go dark gold in the time it takes the inside to just barely soften.

Keep the oil hot and the batches small — a crowded pot drops the temperature, and a slow fry is exactly what lets the cheese win the race against the crust. Any mozzarella that browns and blisters means the oil ran cold; pull the next batch sooner.
