---
title: Grilled Jerk Chicken Thighs
date: 2026-08-04 16:00:05 -0400
description: Bone-in thighs rubbed in a blended scotch bonnet-allspice paste, marinated overnight, then grilled skin-down over two-zone heat until charred and cooked through.
image: /assets/images/Grilled-Jerk-Chicken-Thighs.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 20 min (plus overnight marinate)
cook_time: 30 min
tags: [chicken, grilled, spicy, main]
ingredients:
  - 8 | bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs
  - 6 | scallions, roughly chopped
  - 2 | scotch bonnet peppers, stemmed
  - 3 cloves | garlic
  - 1 tbsp | fresh ginger, grated
  - 2 tbsp | brown sugar
  - 2 tbsp | soy sauce
  - 2 tbsp | lime juice
  - 2 tbsp | vegetable oil
  - 1 tbsp | fresh thyme leaves
  - 2 tsp | ground allspice
  - ½ tsp | ground cinnamon
  - ¼ tsp | ground nutmeg
  - 1 tsp | salt
  - ½ tsp | black pepper
  - To finish | lime wedges and sliced scallion
method:
  - Combine the scallions, scotch bonnet, garlic, ginger, brown sugar, soy sauce, lime juice, oil, thyme, allspice, cinnamon, nutmeg, salt, and pepper in a food processor and blend to a coarse, spoonable paste.
  - Score the skin of each thigh a few times, then rub the paste over and under the skin. Cover and marinate in the fridge at least 4 hours, ideally overnight.
  - Let the chicken sit at room temperature 20 minutes before grilling, and set up the grill for two-zone heat, with coals or one burner banked to one side.
  - Grill the thighs skin-side down over the direct side until deeply charred in spots, about 5 minutes.
  - Move the thighs to the indirect side, cover, and cook until an instant-read thermometer reads 165°F at the thickest point, 20 to 25 minutes more, turning once.
  - Rest 5 minutes, then serve with lime wedges and scallion.
---

The sugar in the marinade is what makes two-zone heat non-negotiable — parked entirely over direct flame, it scorches to bitter black before the meat underneath is anywhere near done. Char the skin hard first, then let the indirect side finish the job slowly.

Wear gloves handling the scotch bonnet, and drop to one pepper (or swap in a habanero) if a full two feels like too much — the heat builds fast once it's blended through the whole marinade rather than left in one bite. Good with rice and peas or grilled plantains, and the paste works just as well on drumsticks or a whole spatchcocked bird if you'd rather skip skewering thighs one at a time.
