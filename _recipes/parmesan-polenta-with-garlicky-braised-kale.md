---
title: Parmesan Polenta with Garlicky Braised Kale
date: 2026-07-27
description: A pot of cornmeal stirred into something creamy, with a skillet of garlicky kale so it isn't just a bowl of starch.
image: /assets/images/Parmesan-Polenta-With-Garlicky-Braised-Kale.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 10 min
cook_time: 40 min
tags: [side, vegetarian]
ingredients:
  - 1 cup | coarse polenta
  - 4 cups | water
  - 1 cup | whole milk
  - 1 tsp | salt, plus more to taste
  - 1 cup | grated Parmesan
  - 3 tbsp | butter
  - 2 tbsp | olive oil
  - 4 cloves | garlic, sliced
  - 1 bunch | kale, stemmed and torn
  - ¼ tsp | red pepper flakes
  - To finish | black pepper
method:
  - Bring the water, milk, and salt to a boil in a heavy pot.
  - Whisk in the polenta in a slow stream, then drop the heat to low.
  - Cook, stirring often and scraping the bottom, until the polenta is thick and starts pulling away from the sides, about 35 minutes.
  - Meanwhile, warm the olive oil in a wide skillet over medium heat and add the garlic and red pepper flakes, cooking until the garlic turns golden at the edges.
  - Add the kale in handfuls, tossing each batch until wilted before adding the next.
  - Cover and cook until the stems are tender when pierced, about 8 minutes, adding a splash of water if the pan looks dry.
  - Stir the Parmesan and butter into the polenta until melted and glossy.
  - Spoon the polenta into bowls and top with the braised kale and black pepper.
---

The ratio is what saves you here — four parts liquid to one part cornmeal keeps the polenta loose enough that a low flame and occasional stirring is all it needs, no standing over the pot with a whisk. If it seizes up before it's fully cooked, a splash of hot water loosens it right back.

Swap the kale for chard or collards depending on what's in the crisper; just adjust the covered cooking time until the stems give easily. Leftover polenta firms up in the fridge and is good sliced and pan-fried the next morning under a fried egg.
