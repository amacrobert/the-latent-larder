---
title: Braised Chickpeas with Harissa and Yogurt
date: 2026-07-25 22:37:32 -0400
description: A vegetarian main that eats like a stew, with none of the standing over the pot.
image: /assets/images/Braised-Chickpeas-With-Harissa-And-Yogurt.webp
servings: 4
prep_time: 15 min
cook_time: 40 min
tags: [vegetarian, main, spicy]
ingredients:
  - 3 tbsp | olive oil
  - 1 | onion, diced
  - 4 cloves | garlic, minced
  - 3 tbsp | harissa paste
  - 1 tsp | ground cumin
  - 1 tsp | ground coriander
  - 2 (15 oz) cans | chickpeas, drained
  - 1 (14 oz) can | crushed tomatoes
  - 1 cup | vegetable broth
  - ½ cup | plain yogurt
  - To finish | lemon zest and parsley
  - Flaky salt, to finish
method:
  - Warm the olive oil in a wide pot over medium heat and cook the onion until soft and translucent, about 6 minutes.
  - Stir in the garlic, harissa, cumin, and coriander and cook until the paste darkens and smells toasted, about 1 minute.
  - Add the chickpeas, crushed tomatoes, and broth, and bring to a simmer.
  - Partially cover and simmer, mashing a few chickpeas against the side of the pot as you go, until the sauce has thickened enough to coat a spoon, about 30 minutes.
  - Season with flaky salt, then swirl in half the yogurt off the heat until just streaked through, not fully mixed in.
  - Spoon into bowls and top with the remaining yogurt, lemon zest, and parsley.
---

The mashed chickpeas are what thicken this, not flour or cream — press a spoon
against a few of them halfway through simmering and the starch does the rest.
Rush the simmer and you get chickpeas in tomato water instead of a stew that
holds together.

White beans work in place of chickpeas if that's what's in the pantry. Serve
with something to mop the pot: a torn piece of focaccia is the obvious choice
if you have a loaf going.
